# Does LDL particle size matter?

The disagreement: is small, dense LDL ("pattern B") intrinsically more
atherogenic than large, buoyant "fluffy" LDL ("pattern A") — or is
particle size a red herring once particle number is counted?

## The case for size

Ron Krauss (UC Berkeley/Donner Lab) showed via gradient gel
electrophoresis, with replication by others including [[jim-otvos]]'s
group in Framingham-era data, that at a given LDL cholesterol level,
people with predominantly small dense LDL have substantially higher
cardiovascular risk — on the order of three-fold. Mechanistic rationales
became standard: smaller particles enter the arterial wall more easily,
bind and are retained more avidly, and oxidize more readily (Peter Attia
MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be
assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## The case against (Otvos/Attia position)

- A smaller LDL particle by definition carries less cholesterol, so at a
  given LDL-C, people with small LDL simply have **more particles**. When
  you stratify instead at a given LDL particle number, size adds no risk
  whatsoever — "not a bit" (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis:
  how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single blood
  sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- Familial hypercholesterolemia is the natural counterexample: FH
  patients have very high particle numbers of **large** LDL and,
  untreated homozygotes, die at 30–35. "Large fluffy LDL is benign" is,
  per Otvos, a fallacious idea (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood
  analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single
  blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- Therapies chosen for making particles bigger (niacin, fibrates, CETP
  inhibitors raising HDL/LDL size) repeatedly failed to show efficacy in
  outcome trials, undercutting size as a treatment target (Peter Attia MD
  — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be
  assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## Status

Both sides agree on the epidemiologic phenomenon (small-LDL predominance
marks higher risk at a given LDL-C); the dispute is about the causal
variable. On current evidence presented here, particle number explains
the size association, and size-based reassurance ("my LDL is all large
and fluffy") is not defensible. See [[ldl-particle-number]] for the
discordance data. Note the practical irony: LipoScience was founded on
measuring LDL size, and its own analyses later showed size doesn't add
independent risk information (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis:
how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single blood sample",
2026-08-03, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
