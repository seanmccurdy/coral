# MVX (metabolic vulnerability index)

A composite mortality-risk score (0–100, higher = worse) derived from a
single NMR blood scan, developed by [[jim-otvos]]'s group. Its six
components: small HDL particle concentration, [[glyca]], citrate, and the
three branched-chain amino acids (leucine, isoleucine, valine). Two
subscores, each also 0–100: IVX (inflammatory vulnerability: GlycA +
small HDL-P) and MMX (metabolic malnutrition: citrate + BCAAs) (Peter
Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be
assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## Discovery: top-down, not mechanism-first

MVX was not built from a mechanistic hypothesis. Otvos's group mines
stored NMR spectra from large longitudinal cohorts (MESA, Women's Health
Study, Framingham Offspring, JUPITER, the Diabetes Prevention Program —
many measured for free in exchange for outcomes access), re-interrogating
old spectra as new markers become measurable. In Duke's CATHGEN
biorepository (~7,000 cardiac-catheterization patients, mean age ~60,
17% five-year mortality — of whom fewer than half died of cardiac
causes), small HDL-P and GlycA strongly predicted death; citrate and
BCAAs added independently, and the composite was published in 2023 with
replication in an independent Utah catheterization cohort (Peter Attia MD
— "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed
from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

The biology was rationalized afterwards via the malnutrition-inflammation
syndrome / protein-energy-wasting literature (kidney disease, dialysis,
heart failure, cachexia, sarcopenia). Notably, *low* BCAAs mark mortality
risk — the opposite direction from their association with insulin
resistance and diabetes risk (see [[lpir-score]]) — plausibly reflecting
mTOR signaling and skeletal-muscle protein turnover (Peter Attia MD —
"402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed
from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## Replication and the age-independence surprise

- Subgroup analyses in CATHGEN: the mortality association holds equally
  in men and women, with/without obesity, diabetes, heart failure, prior
  MI, coronary occlusion, or kidney disease — equally strong if not
  stronger in people without chronic disease (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR
  blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a
  single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- In healthy ~60-year-olds (MESA, disease-free subset), top-vs-bottom
  MVX quartile carried roughly two-to-three-fold mortality difference
  (~1.5–2 hazard ratio per SD adjusted) (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR
  blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a
  single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- Most striking (unpublished, study unnamed, submission pending as of the
  episode): in ~3,000+ healthy 25–30-year-olds sampled ~35 years ago, the
  MVX distribution was nearly identical to that of 60-year-olds, scores
  were stable over decades, MVX was virtually unassociated with age, and
  baseline MVX predicted premature mortality over 35 years of follow-up —
  raising the open question of whether high MVX is acquired or present
  from birth (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality
  risk and more can be assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- In heart failure cohorts with frailty data: physical frailty correlates
  only weakly with MVX (~0.2), and each adds independently to mortality
  prediction; in a separate study of older adults (name unclear in the
  transcript), small HDL-P was the strongest of 186 examined variables for
  predicting two-year mortality (Peter
  Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can
  be assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- A newly published MASLD paper found MVX added predictive value to
  FibroScan for subsequent fibrosis (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood
  analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single
  blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## What MVX measures: dying, not disease

Otvos's interpretation: MVX is "metabolic frailty" — it predicts
susceptibility to dying from whatever disease or insult arrives, rather
than predicting who gets the diseases. Literature associations between
MVX and disease incidence are, he argues, artifacts of composite
endpoints that combine fatal and non-fatal events (Peter Attia MD — "402
‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a
single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## Open questions and applications

Causality, modifiability, and intervention trials remain untested; no one
yet knows whether lowering MVX lowers mortality (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒
NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a
single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)). Proposed uses:
pre-surgical resilience screening, enriching clinical trials for
mortality events, and predicting who survives/responds to aggressive
therapy (Attia's suggestion: response to checkpoint inhibitors like
Keytruda). Practically, MVX comes "along for the ride" from the same 150
µL NMR scan as the lipoprotein measures, at a marginal cost near $1
(Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more
can be assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## Speculative implication for CETP inhibition

Because small HDL-P inversely associates with all-cause mortality, and
potent CETP inhibitors reduce small HDL-P by 10–20% (converting small HDL
particles into large ones), Otvos speculates this could partially offset
the LDL benefit of obicetrapib in its pending outcome trial — pure
speculation until the trial reads out, and possibly one reason earlier
CETP inhibitors disappointed (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis:
how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single blood sample",
2026-08-03, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
