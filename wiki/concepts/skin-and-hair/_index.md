Begin with [[visible-skin-and-facial-aging]] for the layered anatomy of appearance, [[visible-aging-outcomes-and-evidence]] for judging whether an intervention produced an interpretable benefit, [[skin-barrier-and-moisturization]] for skin function, and [[skincare-evidence-and-routine-design]] for routine construction. [[post-weight-loss-skin-laxity]] applies the compartment model to rapid volume change, while [[home-skin-photobiomodulation]] separates device specifications from delivered exposure and outcomes. Do not treat “collagen production” as a complete model of visible aging or imply that a topical can reach every anatomical compartment.

[[topical-visible-aging-interventions]] ranks photoprotection, prescription retinoids, moisturizers, niacinamide, vitamin C, acids, peptides, growth factors, and polynucleotide claims by finished-formula evidence. [[procedures-injectables-and-structural-facial-aging]] maps pigment and vascular treatment, remodeling, neuromodulation, volume replacement, tightening, and surgery to the anatomical layer they can plausibly change.

Existing intervention chapters cover [[photoprotection]], [[topical-retinoids]], [[azelaic-acid]], and [[procedural-skin-remodeling]], but their comparative claims still need to be reorganized around target tissue, patient-important visible outcomes, durability, skin-tone applicability, adverse effects, operator dependence, and evidence quality. [[actinic-purpura-and-aging-skin-fragility]] connects ultraviolet matrix damage, vessel support, bruising, and wound healing on the backs of the hands. [[hair-loss-diagnosis-and-scalp-health]] applies a diagnosis-first framework to follicles and scalp conditions; [[hair-shaft-damage-and-bond-builders]] covers the separate problem of dead-fiber damage and cosmetic reinforcement. [[topical-pdrn-and-centella]] illustrates route-specific evidence and the difficulty of attributing effects in multi-ingredient products.

[[cosmetic-product-safety-and-regulatory-gaps]] applies a consequence-sensitive safety model to periocular drug-like effects, reactive home nail systems, prohibited solvents, cross-border traceability, and packaging-mediated exposure. [[michelle-wong]] records the source-appraisal and formulation framework behind several of these chapters.
