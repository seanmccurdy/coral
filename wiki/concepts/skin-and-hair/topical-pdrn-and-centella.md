# Topical PDRN and Centella extracts

Polydeoxyribonucleotides (PDRN) are purified DNA fragments, described here as roughly 50–1,500 kilodaltons, marketed for hydration, soothing, brightening, repair, and dermal remodeling. Injectable salmon-derived PDRN supplied the category's reputation, but a topical cosmetic is a different intervention: intact stratum corneum, fragment distribution, source, purification, concentration, and vehicle all affect what reaches viable cells. Titrated extract of *Centella asiatica* (TECA) is a standardized mixture enriched in selected triterpenes, commonly asiaticoside, asiatic acid, and madecassic acid; titration makes the active profile more controlled than an unspecified whole-plant extract. ## From ingredient name to skin effect

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  SRC[Animal, plant or marine source] --> PUR[Extraction, purification and standardization]
  PUR --> SIZE[Fragment or triterpene composition]
  SIZE --> FORM[Concentration and finished vehicle]
  FORM --> BARR[Delivery across or within the skin barrier]
  BARR --> CELL[Exposure of viable keratinocytes / fibroblasts]
  CELL --> OUT[Hydration, soothing, repair or remodeling endpoint]
  HYPE[Injectable or traditional-use reputation] -.->|does not establish| OUT
```

DNA from different organisms uses the same four-base sugar-phosphate architecture, so species origin alone does not provide a clear topical mechanism. Salmon origin was selected in part for injectable purity and compatibility; that rationale does not automatically transfer to a rose-, rice-, centella-, or marine-labeled topical. Lab Muffin's distinctive appraisal is that source may matter less on skin than formulation and whatever mechanism the fragments actually use, but the field has not established whether topical variants reproduce the injectable mechanism or work through other effects. This is a biologically plausible hypothesis with preliminary evidence, not a demonstrated equivalence. (@LabMuffinBeautyScience (Lab Muffin Beauty Science) — "New K-Beauty finds: PDRN, TECA, lip tint and more (AD)", 2026-06-19, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HViQVEobv88))

Labels may list cosmetic PDRN as sodium DNA, often without disclosing whether its DNA-fragment mixture came from salmon or trout milt. Most cited research remains in cells or animals, with limited clinical work centered on wound healing and tissue repair rather than ordinary intact-skin cosmetic outcomes. The critical translational steps are therefore unproven: a topical eye cream must deliver relevant fragments across the barrier, expose the intended viable cells, engage the proposed repair pathway, and improve a patient-visible endpoint without consequential off-target signaling. Dr Dray's distinctive position is stronger than simple uncertainty—she calls topical PDRN a gimmick at the present evidence level—while also judging meaningful penetration and activity unlikely enough that existing cosmetic use should not itself be treated as a cancer alarm. This is expert interpretation, not an externally reviewed safety determination. (@DrDrayzday (Dr Dray) — "PDRN Skincare: Is It Worth the Hype? A Dermatologist Explains", 2026-08-24, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bktMjB3Ke2o))

Periocular products also demonstrate why the finished formula, target, and anatomy matter more than the fashionable label. Caffeine may transiently narrow superficial vessels and reduce some vascular darkness or puffiness, while glycerin, hydroxyethyl urea, sodium hyaluronate, hydrolyzed collagen, and adenosine can hydrate or form a surface film; niacinamide may support barrier function and reduce pigment transfer when tolerated. Hydration can scatter light and temporarily make fine surface lines less visible, but no cream replaces orbital bone or fat-volume changes that create age-related hollows and bags. A dedicated eye cream is optional: a tolerated facial moisturizer can moisturize eyelids, while a purpose-designed periocular retinoid, vitamin C product, or caffeine product may be useful when its specific active and gentler formulation match the goal. TECA solves a narrower problem: botanical extracts vary, whereas a titrated extract sets concentrations of chosen constituents. The transcript identifies preclinical and limited clinical work on wound healing, scars, burns, eczema, and possible collagen support, but does not establish broad efficacy for ordinary cosmetic aging. Standardization improves reproducibility of exposure; it does not by itself prove penetration or clinical benefit. (@LabMuffinBeautyScience (Lab Muffin Beauty Science) — "New K-Beauty finds: PDRN, TECA, lip tint and more (AD)", 2026-06-19, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HViQVEobv88))

A dermatologist's independent appraisal lands in the same place while adding a marketing boundary. Centella compounds — madecassoside and asiaticoside are named — have shown promise for improving collagen production both topically and systemically, carry small but interesting evidence for healing after burns and wounds, and even some evidence of wrinkle improvement from centella-containing supplements, making centella a reasonable ingredient with fairly well-established skin benefits and a sensible supporting role beside a retinoid or a well-formulated ascorbic acid. What the evidence does not contain is combination data: there are no good studies of centella paired with retinoids or vitamin C on texture endpoints, and the circulating claim that such combinations improve skin collagen three- to four-fold has never been substantiated anywhere in the literature. The reasonable-adjunct conclusion and the specific multiplier claim deserve opposite treatment. (@DrDrayzday (Dr Dray) — "Can Adapalene Cause Facial Fat Loss? Dermatologist Q&A", 2026-08-23, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DprHVOUpvaM))

Consumer "Cica" product lines are, at bottom, formulations of the same *Centella* constituent family — madecassoside, asiaticoside, madecassic acid, and asiatic acid — positioned for anti-inflammatory, soothing, and possible wound-healing support, typically embedded among humectants, ceramides, niacinamide, and peptides that supply the product's immediate perceivable effects. The attribution rule from the rest of this page applies unchanged: the centella fraction is a plausible soothing adjunct, and the hydration a user feels belongs first to the co-actives. (@DrDrayzday (Dr Dray) — "Dermatologist’s TOP Korean Skincare Picks | What’s Actually Worth It?", 2026-08-21, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x1UCXXw6GU))

A later sponsored review illustrates both strengths and limits of this approach: a cream declared 3% standardized TECA in a liposomal system and combined it with ectoin, niacinamide, adenosine, and multiple Centella derivatives. Standardization narrows composition uncertainty and a delivery system can plausibly alter penetration, but manufacturer absorption, wrinkle, and brightening studies must be matched to the exact current formula, comparator, outcome, and duration. The reviewer's soothing experience cannot isolate TECA from the vehicle and co-actives, and studies on earlier seasonal formulas should not be silently transferred to the current one. (@LabMuffinBeautyScience (Lab Muffin Beauty Science) — "Reviewing popular Korean skincare products (AD)", 2026-01-23, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AjEV51neEak))

Finished formulas complicate attribution. A PDRN serum containing 0.1% liposomal retinal may produce benefits through the established retinoid pathway, while emollients or humectants can explain immediate comfort and hydration. One user's low-stinging experience and sponsor-presented brand testing for elasticity or pores are useful signals for tolerability and hypothesis generation, but they cannot isolate PDRN from retinal, vehicle, expectation, or other ingredients. The correct evidence hierarchy credits the known active while treating PDRN or TECA as an optional, uncertain adjunct. ## Practical implications

- **At purchase: do not buy a topical solely for PDRN hype — moderate evidence-appraisal guidance.** Prefer a formula that also performs a wanted, independently supported function, and judge PDRN or TECA as a possible bonus rather than the reason to displace [[topical-retinoids|retinoid]], moisturizer, or [[photoprotection]]. (@LabMuffinBeautyScience (Lab Muffin Beauty Science) — "New K-Beauty finds: PDRN, TECA, lip tint and more (AD)", 2026-06-19, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HViQVEobv88))
- **When starting: introduce one product at a time and assess comfort over several weeks — moderate.** Stop or reduce frequency for persistent burning, dermatitis, or worsening; a soothing label does not negate irritation from retinal or other actives. (@LabMuffinBeautyScience (Lab Muffin Beauty Science) — "New K-Beauty finds: PDRN, TECA, lip tint and more (AD)", 2026-06-19, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HViQVEobv88))
- **At reassessment after one or two skin-turnover cycles: require a defined outcome — weak evidence for PDRN/TECA cosmetic benefit.** Compare hydration, irritation, pigment, or texture against baseline; do not infer collagen remodeling from transient plumping. (@LabMuffinBeautyScience (Lab Muffin Beauty Science) — "New K-Beauty finds: PDRN, TECA, lip tint and more (AD)", 2026-06-19, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HViQVEobv88))
- **For wounds, burns, eczema, or significant scars: seek clinical diagnosis rather than substituting a cosmetic — strong safety practice.** Preliminary ingredient research is not a treatment protocol. (@LabMuffinBeautyScience (Lab Muffin Beauty Science) — "New K-Beauty finds: PDRN, TECA, lip tint and more (AD)", 2026-06-19, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HViQVEobv88))
- **When applying an eye moisturizer: use only enough for a thin, even film and keep it out of the eye — expert practice guidance.** Dr Dray demonstrates roughly a rice-grain amount per eye; this is an economical and tolerability-oriented heuristic, not a clinically validated dose. ## Gaps & open questions

- Which PDRN fragment sizes, concentrations, sources, and vehicles reach viable human skin after topical application?
- Do topical PDRN products improve validated outcomes beyond their vehicles and co-actives in independent randomized trials?
- Does topical PDRN share any mechanism with injected PDRN, or are observed effects nonspecific hydration and barrier support?
- Which TECA constituent profile and dose is optimal for intact skin, and do wound-healing findings generalize to cosmetic photoaging?
- How reproducible are sponsor-funded elasticity, pore, and soothing claims across skin types and independent laboratories?
- Do centella–retinoid or centella–ascorbic-acid combinations improve any collagen or texture endpoint beyond the established active alone?
- Which causes of periocular darkness or puffiness respond meaningfully to topical caffeine, and how large and durable is the effect?
