# Aging biology

This section explains the molecular and cellular maintenance systems that change with age. Begin with [[hallmarks-of-aging]] for the competing ways of organizing the field and [[aging-dynamics-and-resilience]] for the system-level problem, then read [[stochastic-aging-and-molecular-noise]] for the account that treats accumulating undirected error, rather than any running program, as the substance of aging — the dispute it belongs to is [[programmed-versus-stochastic-aging]]. Study damage and maintenance through [[genomic-instability-and-dna-repair]], [[dream-complex-and-repair-capacity]] for the transcriptional regulation of repair itself, [[loss-of-proteostasis]], [[autophagy-and-lysosomal-quality-control]], and [[mitochondrial-dysfunction]]. Continue through cell-state, matrix, and regenerative consequences in [[cellular-senescence]], [[extracellular-matrix-aging]], [[stem-cell-exhaustion]], [[telomere-biology]], and [[epigenetic-alterations-and-reprogramming]], then follow two routes by which cell state is deliberately manipulated: [[engineered-reprogramming-factors]] rewrites the proteins that drive reprogramming, and [[circulating-rejuvenation-signaling]] treats the aged phenotype as partly a message carried in blood. Use [[biological-age-biomarkers]] to separate measurement from mechanism, [[healthspan-versus-maximum-lifespan]] to separate the outcome classes an intervention could move, and [[longevity-intervention-prioritization]] to translate the biology cautiously.

These mechanisms interact; none is established as a single master cause. Their integration belongs in [[aging-model]].
