# Peptides

A peptide is simply a short chain of amino acids. The word is a chemical
description, not a mark of quality: the category spans some of the most
important drugs ever developed (insulin, GLP-1 agonists) and molecules
with essentially no credible evidence behind them. [[peter-attia]] argues
that one of the biggest marketing successes of the last decade has been
convincing people the word itself implies "natural, safe, and effective"
— when most commercially sold peptides are synthetic, deliberately
modified analogs engineered to bind receptors more tightly, last longer,
or hit new targets (Peter Attia MD — "403 ‒ Peptides: separating
scientific promise from marketing hype", 2026-08-10,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)).

## Five-question evaluation framework

Attia's framework for evaluating any individual peptide (or any drug):

1. **Is there a viable mechanism of action?** A defined mechanism makes a
   claim falsifiable and exposes failure modes. Only ~3% of FDA-approved
   drugs have genuinely unclear mechanisms, so a missing mechanism is an
   early red flag (Peter Attia MD — "403 ‒ Peptides: separating scientific
   promise from marketing hype", 2026-08-10,
   [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)).
2. **Is there evidence of meaningful benefit in humans?** Roughly 30–50%
   of compounds that clear preclinical testing and enter phase 1 trials
   fail to advance to phase 2, often because they don't behave in humans
   as anticipated (Peter Attia MD — "403 ‒ Peptides: separating scientific
   promise from marketing hype", 2026-08-10,
   [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)).
3. **Do we understand safety, dosing, and pharmacokinetics?** How much
   reaches circulation, how long it stays active, what dose was studied,
   what to monitor (Peter Attia MD — "403 ‒ Peptides: separating
   scientific promise from marketing hype", 2026-08-10,
   [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)).
4. **Does the likely benefit justify the risk for this person?** Risk is
   contextual — acceptable for a lethal disease, unacceptable for a
   marginal wellness benefit (Peter Attia MD — "403 ‒ Peptides: separating
   scientific promise from marketing hype", 2026-08-10,
   [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)).
5. **Is there a better-characterized way to get the same result?** If so,
   what is actually gained by the less-characterized version (Peter Attia
   MD — "403 ‒ Peptides: separating scientific promise from marketing
   hype", 2026-08-10, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU))?

## Three evidence buckets

Attia sorts peptides into three buckets (Peter Attia MD — "403 ‒
Peptides: separating scientific promise from marketing hype", 2026-08-10,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)):

- **Bucket 1 — scientifically unsupported**: no validated mechanism,
  little or no credible human evidence, claims that drift over time
  without clinical progress. There is not enough scientific foundation to
  justify use. Example: [[bpc-157]].
- **Bucket 2 — biologically plausible, no human outcome evidence**: a
  credible mechanism, perhaps animal data and real biological activity,
  but no demonstrated improvement in human outcomes; development often
  stalled or was abandoned. Example: [[cjc-1295]].
- **Bucket 3 — scientifically legitimate molecules**: strongest footing,
  but not an endorsement. Evidence belongs to a specific dose, route,
  population, indication, and endpoint — it doesn't travel with the
  molecule — and a legitimate molecule is not the same as a legitimate
  product (see [[gray-market-peptides]]).

## A signature of legitimacy: claims narrow over time

Legitimate drug development narrows uncertainty over time: GLP-1 agonists
earned one indication in rigorous trials, then accumulated others as
evidence arrived. Bad science expands its claims instead — an
ever-growing list of purported benefits with the first claim never nailed
down is the signature of marketing, not a broad mechanism (Peter Attia MD
— "403 ‒ Peptides: separating scientific promise from marketing hype",
2026-08-10, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)). The
companion test: a claim that no observation could prove wrong — where
every disappointing outcome is explained away by dose, timing, supplier,
or stacking — is not a scientific claim (Peter Attia MD — "403 ‒
Peptides: separating scientific promise from marketing hype", 2026-08-10,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)).

## Where the field is headed

Roughly 100 peptide drugs are approved, ~150 are in clinical trials, and
600–700 more are in preclinical development. The most genuine near-term
promise is in metabolism, infectious disease, diagnostics, and cancer,
where peptide specificity is an advantage. Ironically, the uses most
promoted in the wellness world — brain boosting, recovery, tissue repair
— face the steepest scientific climb (blood-brain barrier, biological
complexity of tissue repair) (Peter Attia MD — "403 ‒ Peptides:
separating scientific promise from marketing hype", 2026-08-10,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)).
