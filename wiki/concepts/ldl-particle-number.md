# LDL particle number (LDL-P / ApoB)

The concentration of LDL particles in blood (LDL-P, measured by
[[nmr-lipoprotein-analysis]] in nmol/L) — as distinct from LDL
cholesterol (LDL-C), the mass of cholesterol those particles carry. ApoB
is the closely related immunoassay measure of all ApoB-bearing particles.
The central claim from [[jim-otvos]] and [[peter-attia]]: when LDL-C and
LDL-P disagree, cardiovascular risk tracks with the particle number, not
the cholesterol (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how
mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single blood sample",
2026-08-03, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## The discordance evidence

- Otvos's group introduced concordance/discordance analysis in a 2007
  Framingham paper and a 2011 MESA paper: rank LDL-C and LDL-P as
  percentiles; for the ~concordant majority the two are interchangeable,
  but in discordant groups cumulative cardiovascular events follow LDL-P
  in both directions (high LDL-P/low LDL-C → worse than expected; low
  LDL-P/high LDL-C → better) (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis:
  how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single blood
  sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- Discordance probability rises monotonically with the number of
  metabolic syndrome criteria met — with all five, discordance is
  virtually guaranteed, tying it to insulin resistance (Peter Attia MD —
  "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed
  from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- Mechanism of discordance at low LDL levels: CETP-mediated exchange
  swaps triglyceride into the LDL core in place of cholesteryl ester, so
  as LDL is lowered pharmacologically, LDL-C falls even faster than
  particle number — leaving residual particles that LDL-C understates
  (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and
  more can be assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## Risk assessment vs risk management

An important nuance Otvos concedes: adding LDL-P to standard risk
equations (which already include total and HDL cholesterol, diabetes,
smoking, hypertension) does **not** improve population risk assessment —
likely because low HDL-C associates with high LDL-P and captures the
same signal for non-causal reasons. The stronger case is risk
*management*: on therapy, many patients reach very low LDL-C without
equally low LDL-P/ApoB, and particle number is the better biomarker of
whether LDL-associated risk is actually controlled and whether to
intensify treatment (e.g. add a PCSK9 inhibitor) (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒
NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a
single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

Attia's clinical framing: elevated LDL-C/ApoB/LDL-P is a causal driver
acting over time (like smoking for lung cancer) and should be treated
when it appears, not after disease manifests (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR
blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single
blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## LDL-P vs ApoB

Otvos partners with ApoB advocate Allan Sniderman rather than arguing one
is better: both carry the same message against cholesterol-only
measurement, though NMR additionally resolves triglyceride-rich particle
subspecies that ApoB lumps together. Guideline acceptance of ApoB has
been slow, partly (in Otvos's view) because guideline writers protect
payers; European guidelines moved first (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR
blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single
blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
