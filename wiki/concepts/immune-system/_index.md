# Immune system

[[immune-recognition-and-trafficking]] explains contextual sensing, lymphatic movement, tissue surveillance, and activation thresholds; [[immune-aging-and-rejuvenation]] develops the age-related loss of repertoire and regulation; and [[engineered-cell-therapy-for-solid-tumors]] applies recognition engineering to cancer. [[ebola-virus-disease-and-skin-signs]] shows how acute innate activation, endothelial injury, and coagulation failure become visible at the skin and mucosa.

[[immune-recognition-and-trafficking]] explains how immune cells sense context, negotiate biological continuity, activate, and reach tissues. [[immune-aging-and-rejuvenation]] builds on that machinery to explain repertoire loss, chronic stimulation, altered-self surveillance, inflammatory feedback, and the limits of proposed rejuvenation strategies. [[engineered-cell-therapy-for-solid-tumors]] then takes recognition, trafficking, and suppression as engineering constraints rather than descriptions, showing what has to be added to or removed from a T cell when the native system has already lost the race — and why a compromised patient repertoire pushes manufacturing toward healthy donors. Connect all three to [[cellular-senescence]], [[inflammaging-and-il-6]], [[ai-guided-therapeutic-design]], and [[aging-model]].
