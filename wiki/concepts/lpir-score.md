# LPIR score (lipoprotein insulin resistance)

A composite insulin-resistance score derived from
[[nmr-lipoprotein-analysis]]: six VLDL, LDL, and HDL size and subclass
concentration parameters combined into a 0–100 score (higher = more
insulin resistant). Developed by [[jim-otvos]]'s group as a better
version of the "poor man's" insulin-resistance measure, the
triglyceride/HDL-C ratio (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how
mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single blood sample",
2026-08-03, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## Rationale: treat the cause, not the effect

Otvos's framing parallels cardiovascular prevention: in CVD the causal
factor (cholesterol/ApoB) is easy to measure long before events; in
diabetes it's inverted — the easily measured thing (glucose) is the
*effect*, while the cause (insulin resistance) goes unmeasured. By the
time glucose crosses the 126 mg/dL diabetes threshold, roughly 50% of
beta-cell function is lost, so effective prevention means detecting
insulin resistance while glucose is still normal (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒
NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a
single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)). [[peter-attia]]
adds that the oral glucose tolerance test can catch this years early but
is too cumbersome for routine clinical use (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR
blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single
blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## Evidence

- Validated on longitudinal data to predict development of type 2
  diabetes, and outperforms fasting insulin (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR
  blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a
  single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- Unpublished Diabetes Prevention Program analysis (per Otvos; the
  primary author died before publication): lifestyle intervention lowered
  LPIR significantly, metformin less so, and the *reduction* in LPIR
  powerfully predicted reduced incident diabetes — the interventional
  link supporting causality (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis:
  how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single blood
  sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## Variants and availability

The Diabetes Risk Index (DRI) adds the three branched-chain amino acids
(leucine, isoleucine, valine — elevated BCAAs associate with insulin
resistance) to LPIR; both are commercially available through LabCorp,
LPIR having been offered as a laboratory-developed test alongside the
FDA-cleared LDL-P (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how
mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single blood sample",
2026-08-03, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)). Note
the directional twist relevant to [[mvx]]: *high* BCAAs signal insulin
resistance and diabetes risk, while *low* BCAAs signal mortality risk
(Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more
can be assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
