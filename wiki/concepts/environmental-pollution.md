# Environmental pollution and longevity

The introduction of harmful substances or harmful forms of energy into
the environment — where "harmful" means the exposure can negatively
affect a living system, not merely that it is unnatural, man-made, or
detectable. Beyond air, water, and soil pollution, the category includes
energy/sensory disruptions such as noise, artificial light, and
electromagnetic fields. [[peter-attia]]'s AMA 87 focuses on the physical
exposures of daily life — air, noise, light, EMFs — having covered
chemical pollutants (microplastics, PFAS, phthalates, the main vehicle
for water/soil exposure) in a prior AMA (Peter Attia MD — "Environmental
pollution and longevity: air pollution, noise, light, EMFs, & more (AMA
87 sneak peek)", 2026-07-19,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sDxAiqfy4-g)).

## Why it belongs in a longevity conversation

Some exposures are clearly linked to cardiovascular disease, respiratory
disease, cancer, sleep disruption, cognitive effects, and mortality — air
pollution being the clearest case. The interesting question is not
obvious extremes (wildfire smoke, Chernobyl) but chronic sub-threshold
exposure: living near a highway, cooking indoors with poor ventilation,
sleeping in a noisy urban environment — what the cumulative "area under
the curve" over years and decades implies for disease risk. Unlike
exercise or nutrition, these exposures are only partially under our
control (Peter Attia MD — "Environmental pollution and longevity: air
pollution, noise, light, EMFs, & more (AMA 87 sneak peek)", 2026-07-19,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sDxAiqfy4-g)).

## Why the topic polarizes

The field is so broad (smog, urban glow, cell towers) that signal and
noise blur together; exposures are mostly invisible, accumulate slowly,
and take decades to show health effects, making evidentiary standards
inherently hard to interpret — unlike smoking, where epidemiology
disentangled exposure from disease quickly (Peter Attia MD —
"Environmental pollution and longevity: air pollution, noise, light,
EMFs, & more (AMA 87 sneak peek)", 2026-07-19,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sDxAiqfy4-g)).

## Why it's hard to study rigorously

(Peter Attia MD — "Environmental pollution and longevity: air pollution,
noise, light, EMFs, & more (AMA 87 sneak peek)", 2026-07-19,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sDxAiqfy4-g)):

- **No randomization.** You can't randomize people to 20 years beside a
  freeway vs shepherding in fresh air; only observational "natural
  experiments" exist.
- **Small effect sizes.** Smoking's hazard ratios exceeded 10 —
  interpretable without statistics. Environmental hazard ratios around
  1.3 sit below what epidemiology can confidently assign causality to.
- **Noisy exposure measurement.** Unlike a stable marker (e.g. Lp(a)),
  exposure depends on home, work, commute, ventilation, and how those
  change over time; zip-code-level estimates carry heavy heterogeneity.
- **Exposures travel together.** Highway proximity brings particulate
  matter, nitrogen dioxide, noise, and nighttime light at once —
  disentangling which causes what is largely unresolved.
- **Multifactorial outcomes.** Hypertension or a heart attack also
  reflects genetics, diet, sleep, exercise, and smoking.

## Where it fits in the hierarchy of health

Attia's framing before any specifics: for most people these exposures sit
well below sleep, exercise, nutrition, and what flows from them (blood
pressure, lipids, metabolic health). Buying a $2,000 air filter while
sleeping five hours and not exercising is "a complete inversion of
priorities" — though for someone with everything else dialed in who lives
in demonstrably polluted air, the same purchase may be a remarkable use
of resources. The goal is not eliminating every exposure (metaphysically
impossible) but an 80/20 set of low-burden, high-return interventions,
judged by strength of evidence, size of effect, and modifiability (Peter
Attia MD — "Environmental pollution and longevity: air pollution, noise,
light, EMFs, & more (AMA 87 sneak peek)", 2026-07-19,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sDxAiqfy4-g)).
