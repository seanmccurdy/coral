# GlycA (NMR inflammation marker)

An NMR-derived marker of systemic inflammation. The GlycA signal comes
from glycan (carbohydrate) decoration on circulating acute-phase
proteins; it reflects both the concentration of the four or five most
abundant acute-phase proteins and how heavily glycosylated they are —
glycosylation itself increasing in inflammatory states (Peter Attia MD —
"402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed
from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

Discovered serendipitously: the signal was in the way of a fatty-acid
composition measurement, was quantified so it could be subtracted, and
only then was found to relate strongly to future outcomes — including
mortality — in MESA (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how
mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single blood sample",
2026-08-03, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## Versus CRP

- GlycA is a stable, integrated measure of steady-state systemic
  inflammation, whereas CRP is volatile day-to-day (guidelines recommend
  averaging 2–3 CRP measurements; nobody does). In prediction models
  GlycA typically outperforms CRP, though CRP tends to add independently
  (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and
  more can be assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- Acute infection raises GlycA only ~two-fold (vs up to a thousand-fold
  for CRP), so it is muted but not immune to acute illness (Peter Attia
  MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be
  assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- Correlates strongly with IL-6 and other interleukins; elevated in
  inflammatory diseases (rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis) and responsive
  to anti-inflammatory treatment. It is unspecific and says nothing about
  local inflammation (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how
  mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single blood sample",
  2026-08-03, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

GlycA is one of the six components of the [[mvx]] mortality-risk score
(with small HDL-P it forms the IVX inflammatory subscore) (Peter Attia MD
— "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed
from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
