# BPC-157

A gray-market peptide widely promoted for injury healing, tendon and
ligament repair, gut health, inflammation, pain, and recovery.
[[peter-attia]] calls it "the case study and poster child for everything
that should make you skeptical of a peptide" and places it in his
scientifically-unsupported bucket 1: not enough scientific foundation to
justify use (Peter Attia MD — "403 ‒ Peptides: separating scientific
promise from marketing hype", 2026-08-10,
[link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)).

## Run through the evaluation framework

Applying the [[peptides]] five-question framework (all points from Peter
Attia MD — "403 ‒ Peptides: separating scientific promise from marketing
hype", 2026-08-10, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)):

- **Mechanism: no.** Described as a fragment of a naturally occurring
  "gastric body protection compound," but the alleged parent protein has
  never been fully characterized and BPC-157 doesn't clearly match any
  known human protein. The discoverer has refused to publish the full
  protein sequence ("if you have your own child, you want it to be yours
  forever") or the screening method — details appear deliberately
  withheld. Proposed mechanisms (VEGF/angiogenesis, nitric oxide,
  neurotransmitter systems) are not established in humans, and lacking a
  receptor is not the same as lacking a mechanism.
- **Human benefit: no.** The positive literature is overwhelmingly
  preclinical; more than 80% of published work comes from one academic
  group whose researchers hold IP and commercial interests in the
  molecule. Despite ~three decades of claims, there are no published
  peer-reviewed human randomized trials demonstrating accelerated
  healing.
- **Safety, dosing, pharmacokinetics: no on all three.** Human PK and
  bioavailability are unknown, so promoted dosing protocols are guesses;
  phase 1-style safety characterization has not been done. Absence of an
  obvious safety signal is not evidence of safety.
- **Risk-benefit: poor.** Ironically, if you believe the proposed
  pro-angiogenic VEGF/nitric-oxide mechanisms, you should worry more, not
  less — these are exactly the pathways that potentiate tumor biology,
  abnormal vascular growth, and tissue remodeling. Not proof it causes
  cancer, but a biologically plausible concern set against an
  unquantified benefit.
- **Better-characterized alternatives: yes.** For tendon/ligament
  injuries, pain, inflammation, gut symptoms, and recovery there are
  slower, less marketable, but better-characterized ways to evaluate and
  manage the underlying problem.

## Expanding claims as a red flag

BPC-157's purported benefits have expanded (wound healing → tendons →
ligaments → muscle → gut disease → inflammation → pain → performance →
even multiple sclerosis) while the first claim was never rigorously
demonstrated — the opposite of how legitimate drugs like GLP-1 agonists
earn indications. Attia: at best nobody has bothered to rigorously test
it; at worst somebody did and didn't like what they found (Peter Attia MD
— "403 ‒ Peptides: separating scientific promise from marketing hype",
2026-08-10, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)).

Note against the "can't be patented, so pharma ignores it" argument:
BPC-157 has patents on its salts and production methods (Peter Attia MD —
"403 ‒ Peptides: separating scientific promise from marketing hype",
2026-08-10, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km_01CLEkYU)).
