# Practice playbook

This playbook converts the wiki's current evidence into a cadence. It prioritizes function, quality of life, and established disease prevention over molecular or commercial “age” scores. **Strong** means consistent clinical evidence or established prevention; **moderate** means credible but limited, indirect, or population-dependent evidence; **emerging** means mechanistic, observational, or small-study evidence without established clinical benefit; **contested** means material expert or evidentiary disagreement. Grades apply to the exact claim stated, not to everything associated with an intervention.

This is a scaffold for shared decisions, not a universal prescription. Age, pregnancy, reproductive plans, disability, symptoms, medications, prior disease, access, and local guidelines change the appropriate dose and cadence.

## Daily foundations

- **Avoid tobacco and unnecessary exposure to smoke; use occupational controls for radiation and mutagens — strong.** This reduces defined cancer and cardiopulmonary risks even though it is not proven to reverse organism-wide genomic aging. [[genomic-instability-and-dna-repair]] [[environmental-pollution-and-health]]
- **Accumulate movement and interrupt long fixed positions — strong.** Walk, take stairs, play, or use brief movement breaks; progress from baseline rather than treating a step count as a biological threshold. Triage new pain, modify ordinary non-red-flag loading, and seek assessment for trauma, neurological or systemic signs, progression, or failure to improve. The grade applies to regular movement and sedentary-time reduction, not to a universal step threshold or a specific movement-break protocol. [[daily-movement-mobility-and-pain]]
- **Build meals around minimally processed plant foods, adequate protein, varied fiber sources, and unsaturated fats — strong for risk-factor management.** Make water or unsweetened drinks the default; prefer whole fruit to juice; match energy intake to weight, metabolic health, training, and reproductive needs. Increase fiber gradually when starting low. Keep free sugars modest (below roughly 10% of energy is the population guidance), reduce routine sweet drinks first because they deliver sugar rapidly with little satiety, and — when practical — take an easy ten-minute walk within about half an hour of a high-carbohydrate meal; the walk is **moderate** for the acute glucose excursion, without long-term outcome evidence of its own. ## Weekly training and reserve

- **Perform progressive whole-body resistance training at least twice weekly — strong for strength and function; moderate for exact programming.** Cover push, pull, knee-dominant, hip-dominant, and trunk or carry patterns, train major muscle groups through tolerable ranges, and progress one variable at a time. Most sets need not reach failure. [[resistance-training]] [[skeletal-muscle-hypertrophy]]
- **Include aerobic work across the week and some vigorous effort when appropriate — strong for general activity and fitness, moderate for the optimal distribution.** A time-constrained starting template is two resistance sessions, one short interval session, and one or two easy aerobic bouts. Build intensity gradually when deconditioned or medically complex. At the far upper end — lifelong training around 10 or more hours weekly — observational cohorts report more coronary plaque, including the less stable non-calcified kind, with no event data either way; this does not argue for less exercise at ordinary volumes, but it does argue against letting high training volume substitute for blood-pressure, smoking, and ApoB management. Make sure the week contains genuinely vigorous minutes rather than only light accumulation — **strong for the direction** (a wearable cohort priced one vigorous minute at roughly 4–9 moderate minutes across mortality, cardiovascular, and diabetes outcomes, and the light-activity dose–response plateaus near a 10–15% risk reduction), **moderate for any specific exchange rate**, which is observational arithmetic from one cohort resource. Short deliberate bursts count: three one-to-two-minute "exercise snacks" daily (stair sprints, fast hills, hard bodyweight squats) improve VO2 max and glucose regulation in trials and associate with substantially lower mortality in accelerometer cohorts, and bouts under 10 minutes are legitimate accumulation. Judge weeks by whether they contained vigorous minutes, not by step totals. [[cardiorespiratory-fitness]] [[exercise-intensity-and-health-outcomes]] [[time-efficient-concurrent-training]] [[endurance-exercise-and-coronary-atherosclerosis]]
- **Add safely scaled impact loading for bone when joints, balance, and fracture risk permit — moderate for bone-loading principles and for daily hopping specifically (within-person controlled trials, ~1.8% hip density gain, holds postmenopause, no spinal effect); emerging for the exact jumping dose and fracture prevention.** A reported protocol uses about 10 minutes of varied-direction jumping three times weekly or roughly 50 small jumps daily in short sets; daily single-leg hopping in brief sets (for example after each hour of desk work) is the simplest validated entry step. None of this has fracture-outcome trials. Established osteoporosis, joint disease, or fall risk requires individualized clinical guidance before jumping. For rebuilding meaningful density — especially with diagnosed low bone mass — the evidence-bearing tier is supervised progressive heavy resistance training, not gentle exercise, which lost bone in the randomized comparison arm. [[womens-exercise-across-the-lifespan]] [[bone-remodeling-and-mechanical-loading]] [[resistance-training]]
- **Practice power, balance, and task-specific capacity — moderate.** After a warm-up, use safe fast-concentric work appropriate to experience; practice the actual tasks that matter because general strength and skilled performance are complementary rather than interchangeable. [[muscle-strength-and-mortality]] [[strength-transfer-and-exercise-specificity]]
- **Maintain meaningful cognitive challenge and social contact — moderate.** Choose activities that require attention, correction, learning, or coordination rather than passive repetition. The evidence supports function and reserve more clearly than removal of brain pathology. [[cognitive-reserve-and-brain-health]] [[exercise-enhanced-learning]]
- **Review recurrent food purchases and the week's pattern — moderate.** Compare the portion actually eaten, added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, protein, fiber, and ingredients, then ask what the item replaces. One food's label is less important than repeated exposure. [[food-label-literacy-and-health-halos]]

## Monthly or every 2–4 weeks

- **Review adherence, recovery, pain, performance, sleep, and energy — moderate as a decision process.** Progress training only when the present dose is repeatable; reduce vigorous work first when recovery is failing. Persistent menstrual disruption, unexplained performance loss, injury, or weight change can indicate low energy availability or disease rather than insufficient effort. [[exercise-program-design]] [[low-energy-availability-and-menstrual-function]]
- **During active body-composition change, measure waist or weight under consistent conditions — moderate.** Use trends, not daily noise, and preserve resistance training and adequate protein during intentional loss. Imaging is for a clinical question, not routine self-tracking. [[visceral-and-ectopic-fat]] [[energy-balance-and-calorie-counting]]
- **Review medications, supplements, and off-label longevity products for indication, duplication, interactions, adverse effects, and stopping rules — strong.** The grade applies to medication reconciliation and safety review; evidence for benefit remains product- and indication-specific. Product availability and a plausible mechanism do not establish benefit. [[supplement-evidence-and-safety]] [[experimental-peptides]] [[longevity-clinics-and-evidence]]
- **Do not take beta-carotene or vitamin E supplements to prevent cancer or cardiovascular disease — strong.** Randomized trials show population-specific harm, and USPSTF guidance recommends against both for this purpose. An ophthalmologist-directed AREDS2 formulation for eligible macular degeneration is a separate indication. Do not generalize harmful pharmacologic intravenous vitamin C trials in critical illness to food-level intake, but do not use antioxidant potency as a reason to megadose. [[supplement-evidence-and-safety]]
- **If using a topical retinoid, reassess tolerance and the target condition after a sustained trial — moderate.** Start at a tolerable frequency, support the barrier, and do not interpret persistent inflammatory lesions automatically as “purging.” [[topical-retinoids]]

## Yearly preventive review

- **Review blood pressure, smoking and alcohol exposure, family history, medications, metabolic risk, standard lipids, glucose status, vaccination, mental health, sensory function, and age-appropriate screening — strong.** Add a test only when its result can change a defined decision. Weigh alcohol more heavily when metabolic risk factors or hepatic steatosis are present: the combination is a multiplicative liver exposure (steatosis plus roughly three or more daily drinks carried ~15-fold liver-specific mortality in NHANES, while steatosis alone carried none), and normal liver enzymes do not exclude early metabolic liver disease — **moderate, observational plus mechanism** [[metabolic-liver-disease]]. Repeat surprising results under appropriate conditions before treatment. A normal clinic blood pressure does not exclude masked (occult) hypertension, in which 24-hour, daytime, or nighttime averages are elevated; ambulatory monitoring is the way to detect it, and it is worth considering in people with unexplained vascular findings or very high endurance-training volumes — **moderate**. Base blood-pressure decisions on a proper home average (rested and seated, morning and evening readings for a week, first day discarded) rather than one clinic reading — **strong measurement principle** — and set the treatment target by frailty and history with a clinician rather than by age: robust people including fit elders benefited from intensive (~120) targets in randomized trials, while frailty or orthostatic dizziness argues for ~130–140 and a medication review — **moderate-to-strong**. Isometric holds such as wall sits are a validated adjunct lowering lever (~8 mmHg systolic, pooled randomized trials) — **moderate**. [[blood-pressure-targets-and-frailty]] [[proactive-health-monitoring]] [[cognitive-reserve-and-brain-health]] [[endurance-exercise-and-coronary-atherosclerosis]]
- **Assess long-term atherogenic-particle exposure and absolute cardiovascular risk — strong for the causal pathway, individualized for treatment thresholds.** ApoB can refine particle burden; Lp(a) is generally measured once because inherited level is relatively stable. If treatment is indicated, outcome-supported statins, [[ezetimibe]], or [[pcsk9-inhibition]] may be considered according to risk, response, tolerance, and access. [[lipoprotein-retention-and-atherogenesis]]
- **Review physical capacity and fall or injury risk — moderate.** Track a small repeatable set of strength, power, balance, and aerobic measures rather than chasing unvalidated percentile cutoffs. For adults 65 or older at increased fall risk, refer to exercise emphasizing gait, balance, functional, and resistance training—**strong, guideline-supported**. Audit exercise risk–reward and substitute lower-risk movements that preserve the intended stimulus. [[muscle-strength-and-mortality]] [[exercise-program-design]] [[bone-health-osteoporosis-and-fracture-prevention]]
- **Review hearing and vision, especially when function or cognition changes — strong for sensory function, moderate for dementia-risk reduction.** Correct remediable impairment rather than waiting for social withdrawal. [[cognitive-reserve-and-brain-health]]
- **During perimenopause or menopause, review symptoms, bleeding, sleep, mood, cognition, bone risk, and cardiometabolic risk clinically — strong.** In a typical presentation at age 45 or older, do not use estradiol or other ovarian tests to identify the transition; a single normal FSH or estradiol result does not exclude it. Reserve FSH confirmation mainly for ages 40–45 with symptoms or suspected ovarian insufficiency under 40. Menopause hormone therapy is symptom- and risk-directed, not universal anti-aging replacement; review benefit and harm at least annually when used. [[perimenopause-assessment-and-testing]] [[menopause-hormone-therapy]]
- **For postmenopausal vaginal dryness, painful sex, urinary urgency or frequency, or recurrent urinary tract infections: ask specifically about genitourinary syndrome of menopause and local treatment — strong for symptom-directed assessment; moderate for the route-specific treatment synthesis pending full evidence review.** Local estrogen, vaginal DHEA, or an estradiol ring is distinct from systemic hormone therapy and may require ongoing use. A breast-cancer history, especially current aromatase-inhibitor or anti-estrogen treatment, requires shared decision-making with the relevant specialists because expert reassurance, regulator class warnings, products, doses, and oncology guidance do not reduce to a universal yes or no. [[genitourinary-syndrome-of-menopause]]
- **Review reproductive goals before options narrow — moderate.** Persistent pelvic pain, painful intercourse, heavy bleeding, or difficulty conceiving deserves diagnosis-directed assessment; age-related oocyte aneuploidy and fertility-preservation decisions should not be postponed on the assumption that ovarian rejuvenation is established. ## Age-, risk-, and symptom-triggered screening

These are periodic rather than universally annual. Follow current local guidance and individual risk.

- **Colorectal cancer:** begin established screening at the applicable age and interval; the current source uses age 45 for average risk. Start earlier for relevant family history, inherited risk, inflammatory bowel disease, or concerning symptoms. A positive stool or other non-colonoscopy test requires colonoscopy. **Strong.** [[colorectal-cancer-prevention-and-screening]]
- **Breast cancer:** complete formal risk assessment by early adulthood and use risk, density, family history, and local guidance to choose modality and interval. The wiki supports mammographic screening from 40 for average risk but records that annual versus biennial cadence is not settled by an interval RCT; high-risk people may need earlier MRI-based surveillance. New symptoms require diagnostic assessment regardless of a recent screen. **Strong for screening; contested for exact average-risk interval and supplemental imaging boundaries.** [[breast-cancer-screening]]
- **Bone health:** screen all women aged 65 or older and postmenopausal women younger than 65 who are at increased risk on clinical assessment; population-screening evidence in men is insufficient, while prior fragility fracture or a secondary cause warrants evaluation outside that calendar. A hip or vertebral fragility fracture is a sentinel event and can justify treatment regardless of T-score. Use DXA and absolute fracture risk as decision tools, not as the whole disease. Bisphosphonates are usual first-line pharmacotherapy for primary osteoporosis; very-high-risk disease may warrant anabolic-first sequencing. Never stop denosumab or complete anabolic/romosozumab therapy without a planned antiresorptive transition. **Strong for the women-specific screening recommendation, sentinel-fracture pathway, and transition safety; individualized for men and treatment selection.** [[bone-health-osteoporosis-and-fracture-prevention]] [[perimenopause-assessment-and-testing]] [[bone-remodeling-and-mechanical-loading]]
- **Cardiovascular imaging:** calculate and treat conventional risk before advanced imaging. Do not adopt universal recurring coronary CT angiography; use shared decision-making about whether a result would change care, radiation, contrast, incidental findings, and cost. **Contested for asymptomatic screening.** [[coronary-cta-screening-asymptomatic]] [[coronary-ct-angiography]]
- **Cognitive or neurological change:** seek domain-specific clinical assessment rather than relying on a consumer brain-age score. The 2024 Alzheimer's Association criteria permit biological diagnosis using a sufficiently accurate validated Core 1 biomarker, while clinical stage remains separate; this does not make any positive consumer blood amyloid result diagnostic or justify population screening. Medication effects, sensory loss, menopause-related impairment, Lewy-body disease, and mixed pathology remain different possibilities. **Strong for evaluation; contested for asymptomatic biomarker-defined diagnosis and screening.** [[alzheimers-spectrum-and-diagnosis]] [[alzheimers-diagnosis-biological-vs-clinical]] [[lewy-body-disease-and-synucleinopathies]]
- **Confirmed early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease:** discuss anti-amyloid immunotherapy only after biomarker confirmation and an eligibility, MRI, APOE-genotype, comorbidity, anticoagulation, monitoring-burden, and goals-of-care review. These drugs lower amyloid and modestly slow decline in selected trial populations; they do not restore lost cognition, apply to asymptomatic screening, or constitute general anti-aging treatment. **Strong for target engagement; moderate for modest clinical slowing in eligible patients; strong that ARIA and treatment burden require structured monitoring.** [[anti-amyloid-immunotherapy]] [[alzheimers-spectrum-and-diagnosis]]
- **After a kidney stone:** drink to a measured urine output of about 2.5 L/day on schedule rather than thirst, moderate dietary sodium and animal protein, and restrict only genuinely high-oxalate foods when urinary oxalate is high. For recurrent stones or a first stone with risk factors, obtain two baseline 24-hour urine collections and repeat after any medication or diet change; thiazides and potassium citrate have randomized recurrence-reduction evidence. Stone pain with fever or inability to urinate is an emergency; observe an uncomplicated ureteral stone no longer than 4–6 weeks. **Strong for fluid; moderate for diet and urine-directed therapy.** [[kidney-stones]] (@RenaMalikMD (Rena Malik, M.D.) — "How to Fight Kidney Stones and Win Every Single Time", 2026-07-10, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HRPLLdVfiXk))
- **For diagnosed gout:** use clinician-directed urate-lowering treatment to a measured serum-urate target rather than relying on food restriction or treating attacks alone; standard guidance uses below about 0.36 mmol/L, with prescriber-selected flare prophylaxis during initiation and reassessment over the first 3–6 months. Kidney function, interactions, ancestry-related hypersensitivity risk, tophi, and flare history alter the regimen. Weight loss or a GLP-1-associated urate fall prompts medication review, not automatic allopurinol withdrawal; using a GLP-1 drug as primary gout therapy remains unsupported. **Strong for treat-to-target urate lowering and initiation prophylaxis; moderate for review during major weight change; contested/emerging for GLP-1 substitution.** [[gout-and-urate-lowering]] [[glp-1-receptor-agonists]]
- **Possible PCOS:** a woman with two of — persistently irregular or ≥35-day-spaced cycles, hirsutism or persistent androgenic acne, polycystic ovaries on any ultrasound — should ask explicitly whether polycystic ovary syndrome fits, and a confirmed diagnosis makes cardiometabolic surveillance (blood pressure, lipids, glucose status, weight distribution; insulin assessment where it changes a decision) a lifelong cadence that intensifies rather than lapses at menopause. **Strong as a diagnosis-seeking and surveillance principle (the syndrome is underdiagnosed until fertility is at stake and carries established long-term diabetes and cardiovascular risk); moderate for insulin-first testing; the specific age-45 CAC trigger stays investigational on the topic pages.** [[polycystic-ovary-syndrome]] [[insulin-resistance]] [[proactive-health-monitoring]] (@hubermanlab (Andrew Huberman) — "Essentials: How to Optimize Female Hormone Health for Vitality & Longevity | Dr. Sara Gottfried", 2026-08-13, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wbuPQPu-03Y))
- **Persistent erectile change:** obtain cause-directed vascular, metabolic, sleep, medication, hormonal, neurological, and psychological assessment rather than assuming normal aging. **Moderate.** [[erectile-dysfunction-and-vascular-health]]
- **New persistent skin, nail, or itch findings:** new scleral yellowing, severe or new generalized itch without a rash, a characteristic violaceous rash with proximal muscle weakness, new digital clubbing, a persistent velvety fold plaque, eruptive xanthomas, or spoon-shaped nails deserve diagnosis-led clinical evaluation rather than scrubbing, cosmetic removal, or self-ordered broad panels; a velvety fold plaque specifically prompts insulin-resistance assessment, and eruptive xanthomas prompt lipid and medication review. **Strong as a safety and escalation principle; sign-specific predictive values are unquantified and the source page awaits evidence review.** [[cutaneous-signs-of-systemic-disease]] [[insulin-resistance]] [[proactive-health-monitoring]]
- **Possible skin cancer:** an inflammatory-appearing lesion (pimple, insect bite, ingrown hair) that has not healed within a month, a spot that bleeds with trivial contact such as face washing, or a new or changing mole that stands out from its neighbors (the ugly-duckling sign) deserves prompt dermatologic evaluation — early keratinocyte cancer and melanoma are highly curable. New moles can appear normally to roughly age 35–40; change and outlier status, not novelty, are the flags. People with immunosuppression (transplant recipients, immunodeficiency, long-term immunosuppressive therapy) should treat periodic full-skin examination as part of routine care given their greatly multiplied risk. **Strong as a safety and escalation principle (standard dermatologic teaching via a subspecialist source); population-screening trade-offs remain guideline-contested.** [[skin-cancer-risk-and-immune-surveillance]] (@maxlugavere (Max Lugavere) — "The Skin Doctor: Hair Loss, Itchy Skin, Sunscreen Myths, and Anti-Aging Truths", 2026-07-15, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99LCstvkNVw))
- **New or changed headache:** a thunderclap or sudden maximal-intensity headache, a major change in pattern, fever or rash, neurological deficit or confusion, pregnancy-associated new headache, onset with exertion/cough/sex, strong positional dependence, or a painful red eye requires timely urgent assessment rather than trigger tracking or routine rescue medication. For recurrent headache without these red flags, keep a structured diary for at least eight weeks and seek diagnosis-led acute and preventive care. **Strong safety guidance; moderate-to-strong diagnostic guidance.** [[primary-headache-disorders]] (@PeterAttiaMD (Peter Attia MD) — "406 ‒ Migraine, cluster headache, and tension headache: symptoms, causes, prevention, and treatment", 2026-08-31, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uiNEP8A34SE))
- **Symptoms override calendars:** rectal bleeding, unexplained iron-deficiency anemia or weight loss, a new breast symptom, unexpected postmenopausal bleeding, progressive neurological change, chest symptoms, or other red flags require timely diagnostic care rather than waiting for routine screening. **Strong.** [[proactive-health-monitoring]]

## Optional interventions with bounded evidence

- **If heat exposure is accessible and well tolerated, traditional sauna for roughly 20 minutes two to four times weekly can be used as an adjunct — moderate for blood-pressure and vascular intermediate endpoints; emerging for mortality and dementia.** The apparent outcome dose-response comes from Finnish cohorts rather than randomized event trials, so four to seven weekly sessions is not a proven target and heat does not replace aerobic or resistance training. Do not chase extreme temperatures. Hydrate, stop for dizziness or nausea, avoid alcohol and intoxicants around heat, and do not enter while dehydrated or stack sauna with laxative or parasite cleanses, diuretic-driven fluid loss, prolonged heat exercise, or acute illness. Seek individualized guidance with pregnancy, unstable cardiovascular disease, syncope, or medicines that impair thermoregulation, electrolytes, or blood pressure; do not stop prescribed medication on your own. Hot baths may reproduce some hemodynamic effects, but equivalence across heat methods is not established. [[sauna-and-deliberate-heat-exposure]]
- **Use a small handful of unsalted nuts most days as a substitution when tolerated — moderate for the dietary-pattern recommendation; observational for mortality.** Roughly 15 g/day captures most of the cohort dose–response association, and walnut cohorts support about 28 g three to four times weekly, but neither estimate is a proven optimum. Replace refined snacks or saturated-fat-rich foods rather than adding nuts automatically when energy balance matters. [[nut-consumption-and-mortality]] [[dietary-fat-quality-and-cardiovascular-risk]]
- **Tomato products a few times weekly are a reasonable whole-food choice — moderate observational evidence for modest cardiovascular benefit.** Cooked tomatoes, sauce, paste, and juice can raise lycopene exposure, but isolated lycopene has not shown comparable long-term outcome evidence and should not be used as plaque or lipid therapy. The same bound covers lutein: leafy greens and eggs are the evidence-bearing route, and lutein supplements have null cardiovascular-outcome evidence including a ~5-year randomized trial. [[tomato-products-and-lycopene]] [[lutein]]
- **Anthocyanin-rich foods (blueberries, elderberries, chokeberries, blackcurrants, blackberries, red cabbage, purple sweet potato) are a reasonable whole-food component, spread across sources — moderate observational evidence at the nutrient-class level; not a cardiovascular therapy.** Blueberry-specific cohort evidence goes null after multivariate adjustment and trials move only endothelial function, with a possible dose threshold near one cup (~150 g) daily; do not let any berry displace blood-pressure, ApoB, or activity management. [[anthocyanins-and-cardiovascular-health]]
- **Meet calcium needs from food (dairy, leafy greens, fish); do not take calcium supplements for fracture prevention without a clinician-identified indication — strong for the null fracture benefit and consistent with US guidance; the cardiovascular harm signal is credible but less settled.** Do not stop a clinician-prescribed supplement unilaterally. A single spot urinary-iodine test or a normal TSH does not establish personal adequacy. ## Do not convert these mechanisms into a routine

- **Deliberate cold exposure:** no protocol has shown lower disease incidence, mortality, or slower aging. Brief cold can alter alertness, soreness, catecholamines, brown-fat imaging, or short-horizon readiness, but those endpoints do not establish durable benefit; cold-water immersion after resistance training can blunt hypertrophy. Do not schedule it reflexively after lifting when muscle gain matters, and account for cold-shock, arrhythmia, and drowning risk. **Moderate for acute effects and the hypertrophy trade-off; emerging for durable benefit; strong evidence boundary against a longevity claim.** [[deliberate-cold-exposure]] [[exercise-recovery-and-readiness]] [[skeletal-muscle-hypertrophy]]
- **Rapamycin:** strong mouse longevity evidence, unproven human longevity benefit, uncertain dosing, and real adverse effects; weekly dosing cannot be assumed to preserve exercise adaptation. **Emerging/contested.** [[mtor-and-rapamycin]]
- **Senolytics, thymus-regeneration regimens, enzymatic deglycation, plasma exchange, young-plasma fractions, and reprogramming products:** no established healthy-human regimen improves function, prevents disease, or extends life. Some have biological activity or preclinical promise, but cadence cannot be specified. **Emerging, with strong caution against self-treatment.** [[cellular-senescence]] [[thymus-regeneration]] [[enzymatic-deglycation]] [[therapeutic-plasma-exchange]] [[plasma-derived-extracellular-particles]] [[epigenetic-alterations-and-reprogramming]]
- **NAD precursors (NR, NMN), oral NAD, and NAD IV drips:** no healthy-person regimen improves function, prevents disease, or extends life. A small peripheral-artery-disease NR trial is promising but unreplicated and does not admit a consumer dose; changing blood NAD is not a validated surrogate. Avoid IV wellness use, pregnancy/lactation use, and self-treatment during serious disease; consumer blood-NAD targets are unvalidated. **Emerging for benefit, with strong caution against self-treatment.** [[nad-supplementation]] [[nad-precursors-and-healthy-aging]] [[nad-metabolism]]
- **Ketamine and psychedelics:** do not use them recreationally or unsupervised; response is idiosyncratic even under maximal clinical preparation, ketamine is a dissociative anesthetic with addiction potential, and durable single-session benefit cannot be predicted or presumed. Clinical use (monitored ketamine/esketamine for dangerous depression; investigational psilocybin and MDMA indications) is a specialist decision, not a wellness practice. **Strong caution against self-directed use; emerging/contested for clinical efficacy beyond the acute ketamine effect.** [[ketamine-and-psychedelic-therapy]]
- **Nattokinase:** do not substitute it for established cardiovascular prevention; bleeding risk and absent event trials prevent an evidence-based preventive cadence. **Emerging.** [[nattokinase]]
- **Vitamin K2 (MK-7) for arterial calcification:** do not take it to lower a calcium score. The published VitaK-CAC trial (two years, symptomatic coronary disease) slowed calcium progression but did not slow total plaque — total plaque volume grew at least as fast under MK-7 (about 42% versus 67%, difference not statistically significant), consistent with the same plaque simply staying less calcified, which is the pessimistic branch of the surrogate problem since calcified plaque is the more stable phenotype. No trial in this literature is powered for cardiovascular events (the one event-reporting trial had ~13 events, null overall). Anyone on warfarin must coordinate any vitamin K change with the prescriber. **Emerging, with the outcome question unanswered and the surrogate case weakened.** [[vitamin-k2]]
- **Myostatin-pathway antibodies (apitegromab, bimagrumab) to protect muscle during weight loss:** investigational and trial-only; body-composition endpoints move while muscle-function endpoints (physical function, grip strength) have not. Resistance training with adequate protein remains the muscle-preserving measure with established functional evidence, and no trial has compared the two. **Emerging; not a self-directed option.** [[myostatin-pathway-inhibition]] [[glp-1-receptor-agonists]]
- **Generic probiotics and “gut cleanses”:** effects are product-, outcome-, and context-specific; do not substitute them for fiber, colorectal screening, or validated weight management. **Emerging/contested.** [[probiotics-prebiotics-and-postbiotics]] [[food-patterns-and-gut-ecology]]
- **Multi-cancer early-detection blood tests and whole-body MRI screening:** do not replace site-specific screening or treat either as established population care; the NHS multi-cancer trial failed its primary endpoint, and whole-body MRI in asymptomatic people finds cancer in about 1.57% with no evidence of longer or better life, a ~3-in-10 uncertain-finding rate, and a documented treatment cascade. A fully informed self-funded choice remains a personal decision, but it should never follow a recommendation from a clinician with a referral-fee interest. **Emerging/contested.** [[multi-cancer-early-detection]] [[cancer-screening-and-overdiagnosis]]
- **NMR-derived composite scores (MVX, LPIR):** do not start a drug, supplement, or procedure from a metabolic-vulnerability or insulin-resistance score alone. ApoB or LDL particle number legitimately clarifies atherogenic burden at routine risk-appropriate testing, and LPIR can be considered when earlier insulin-resistance detection would change a defined decision, but MVX mortality prediction is emerging-to-moderate cohort evidence with no demonstration that lowering the score improves outcomes, and LDL particle size should never be treated on its own. **Strong for the ApoB pathway; emerging for the composite scores.** [[nmr-blood-analysis]] [[lipoprotein-retention-and-atherogenesis]]
- **Consumer continuous glucose monitoring in normoglycemia:** do not wear a CGM to suppress ordinary post-meal rises when established testing shows normal glycemic control — the 23-study review found no appreciable glycemic benefit in healthy normoglycemic users, no BMI change, and no durable outcome evidence, and a single excursion is the summed response to many inputs, not a surrogate for future disease. The bounded exception is prediabetes, where CGM may serve as an adjunct to a structured program with a pre-defined behavioral question, interpretation rule, and conventional follow-up measure; stop the experiment if it produces anxiety, progressively restrictive eating, or no decision change, and never adjust medication from an over-the-counter sensor without professional review. **Moderate evidence against routine healthy-person use; emerging for the prediabetes adjunct.** [[continuous-glucose-monitoring]] [[proactive-health-monitoring]] [[free-sugars-and-glycemic-response]]
- **Commercial biological-age tests:** if used, hold platform and conditions constant, repeat only after a meaningful interval, and demand change beyond technical error. Never use one score to justify treatment. **Moderate as research measurement; absent evidence for consumer-guided optimization.** [[biological-age-biomarkers]]

## Where experts disagree

- **Diet:** energy balance governs body-energy change, but calorie counts are noisy and food structure, appetite, timing, and adherence determine real-world intake. Meal timing does not outrank nutritional adequacy or sustainable energy balance. [[energy-balance-and-calorie-counting]] [[caloric-restriction-and-meal-timing]]
- **Seed oils:** residual-hexane harm at dietary exposure is poorly supported; the effect of very high linoleic-acid intake and repeatedly heated oils remains a separate question. Always specify the replacement fat. [[seed-oils]]
- **Ketogenic diets and ApoB:** favorable glucose or triglycerides should not be assumed to neutralize a large ApoB increase; the magnitude of residual risk in metabolically healthy ketogenic populations remains contested. [[ketogenic-diet-apob-and-atherosclerosis]]
- **Menopause hormones:** symptom treatment and bone-loss prevention while used are distinct from universal longevity claims. FDA's 2026 boxed-warning changes reduce category-wide alarm but do not erase product-specific risks or create cardiovascular, dementia, or longevity indications. WHI interpretation still depends on population, formulation, route, timing, and outcome. [[whi-and-menopause-hormone-therapy]] [[menopause-hormone-therapy]]
- **Breast, bone, coronary, and Alzheimer's screening:** earlier detection may help selected high-risk people, but broader testing can create false positives, overdiagnosis, radiation or procedural burden, and treatment without outcome benefit. [[breast-cancer-screening]] [[perimenopause-assessment-and-testing]] [[coronary-cta-screening-asymptomatic]] [[alzheimers-diagnosis-biological-vs-clinical]]
- **Vitamin D targets:** everyone agrees measured deficiency is worth correcting; the disagreement is where sufficiency ends. Major nutrition bodies use 20 ng/mL adequacy and common lab convention treats ≥30 as sufficient, while the insufficiency-first camp (notably Rhonda Patrick) advocates titrating to 40–60 ng/mL or higher. No outcome trial supports the higher range in healthy people, large supplementation trials are broadly null on primary endpoints, and high-dose regimens carry falls and fracture harm signals — which is why this playbook grades correction as the action and the upper range as contested. [[vitamin-d]] [[rhonda-patrick]] [[supplement-evidence-and-safety]]
- **Statin-associated dysglycemia:** the cardiovascular event benefit is established for indicated patients, while average glucose effects are small and heterogeneous. Experts differ on how much to attribute to a specific statin versus intensity, dose, baseline insulin resistance, and detection; the disagreement supports risk-directed monitoring and shared adjustment, not abandonment of ApoB lowering. [[statins-and-glycemic-risk]]
- **Exercise selection:** strength capacity transfers broadly, skill transfers specifically, and pain or injury changes the risk–benefit calculation. No single “functional” exercise is universally required. [[strength-transfer-and-exercise-specificity]] [[muscle-damage-and-hypertrophy]]

## Priority order when time, money, or attention is scarce

1. Address urgent symptoms, tobacco, uncontrolled blood pressure or metabolic risk, harmful substance use, and missed established screening.
2. Build repeatable sleep, movement, resistance training, aerobic fitness, and a nutritionally adequate dietary pattern.
3. Treat documented disease risk with outcome-supported care and monitor response.
4. Add low-burden optional interventions only for a defined endpoint.
5. Do not let speculative drugs, supplements, procedures, scans, or age tests displace the first four levels. [[longevity-intervention-prioritization]]

## Gaps & open questions

- Which minimum training and dietary doses preserve function across disability, aging, and major hormonal transitions?
- Which screening expansions improve mortality or quality of life after false positives, overdiagnosis, and downstream procedures are counted?
- Which biomarker bundles change decisions beyond established risk models?
- Which behavioral skills produce durable functional benefit rather than short-term symptom change?
- Which supplement effects are formulation-specific, and which replicate across independent trials?
- How should multiple simultaneous interventions be evaluated without losing attribution or missing interactions?
