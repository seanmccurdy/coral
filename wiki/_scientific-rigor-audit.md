# Scientific-rigor audit

Audit opened: 2026-08-12

## Executive assessment

Coral has strong editorial instincts—many pages distinguish mechanism from outcome, warn against extrapolation, and preserve uncertainty—but it is not yet a fully fact-checked clinical textbook. Its principal weakness is evidence provenance: most chapters cite podcasts or videos showing where an idea entered the wiki but do not yet cite the guidelines, systematic reviews, randomized trials, or primary peer-reviewed human studies needed to validate the claim itself.

The current corpus contains 164 substantive pages. At audit opening, 152 had never received an independent evidence review, 11 contained a scholarly `## References` section, and 12 were marked `current`. A page without scholarly references is not necessarily false; it is **not yet independently verified**. Until review is complete, its evidence adjectives and practical protocols must not be treated as validated merely because they are written cautiously or attributed to a credentialed speaker.

## Audit standard

Each page is evaluated at three levels:

1. **Concept accuracy:** definitions, prevalence, mechanisms, causal language, and generalizability.
2. **Outcome evidence:** whether effects occur in humans and whether endpoints are biomarkers, symptoms, function, disease events, quality of life, healthspan, or lifespan.
3. **Protocol validity:** population, indication, dose, timing, cadence, comparator, contraindications, adverse effects, monitoring, stopping rules, and consistency with professional guidance.

Evidence is prioritized in this order: current regulator labeling and professional guidelines; systematic reviews and meta-analyses; pivotal randomized trials; prospective human studies; mechanistic human research; animal and cell studies; expert interpretation; commercial claims. Podcast citations document provenance only.

## Finding classes

- **Supported:** wording and confidence match the best available evidence.
- **Qualified but unverified:** plausibly correct and cautiously written, but scholarly support has not been independently checked.
- **Overstated:** direction may be plausible, but certainty, effect size, population, or protocol exceeds the evidence.
- **Unsupported:** no adequate evidence was identified for the material claim.
- **Contradicted:** higher-quality or newer evidence conflicts with the claim.
- **Potentially unsafe or clinically misleading:** wording could alter care, delay diagnosis, encourage inappropriate treatment, or obscure important harms.

## Confirmed material correction

### Adenomyosis pretreatment before frozen embryo transfer — P0

The chapter previously said that two to four months of GnRH suppression before frozen transfer improves implantation, miscarriage, and live birth, grading the protocol `moderate-to-strong`. Recent adenomyosis-specific systematic reviews do not demonstrate a live-birth benefit before frozen embryo transfer; favorable associations are largely observational and heterogeneous. ASRM describes the evidence as limited and allows consideration in selected recurrent-implantation-failure cases rather than endorsing a routine protocol. The page now presents the evidence as contested/low certainty and cites the reviews and ASRM guidance. [[adenomyosis]]

## Cross-wiki defects

1. **Podcast evidence leakage:** `strong` or `moderate-to-strong` often appears beside only a video citation. Every grade must attach to underlying scholarly evidence.
2. **Unvalidated precision:** exact doses, thresholds, ages, percentages, and monitoring intervals require nearby citations and population qualifiers.
3. **Expert-practice inflation:** a personal protocol, open-label series, or center practice is not a general procedural requirement.
4. **Surrogate substitution:** clocks, lipids, glucose, imaging, immune markers, or molecular pathways are not healthspan or lifespan outcomes.
5. **Preclinical translation:** animal and cell findings require model, endpoint, replication, and human-translation boundaries in the same paragraph.
6. **Metadata mismatch:** `current` must mean independently reviewed, not recently written. Synthesis cannot be fully current while most dependencies are unreviewed.
7. **Missing negative evidence:** null trials, adverse findings, retractions, and stronger contradictory reviews need equal visibility.

## Completion criteria

A page leaves the queue only when material concept, outcome, and protocol claims have nearby authoritative citations; grades match design and endpoint; contradictory and null evidence is represented; safety and applicability are explicit; frontmatter records a real cutoff; and material changes are propagated to synthesis. Until then, it remains `review-due` or `under-review`.

## Current state

The wiki is strongest as a structured, uncertainty-aware map of topics and expert provenance. It is not yet uniformly reliable as a clinical reference. The highest-value next step is converting P0 and P1 from video-supported exposition into claim-level scholarly synthesis, while pruning claims that cannot survive that process.
