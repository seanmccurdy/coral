# Hypothesis idea nursery

This bounded queue contains creative but unreviewed human-direct ideas. Entries are research prompts, not conclusions, advice, or invitations to self-experiment. The weekly workshop generates three seeds unless twelve are active, then may promote at most one after scholarly review. Scores run from 1 (low) to 5 (high); higher evidence distance means more missing inferential steps. Active seeds are ranked by overall workshop priority: direct human value and leverage first, then discrimination, feasible testing, evidence distance, and safety; scores inform the ordering but are not added into a single arithmetic total.

## Active seeds

### 1. Post-meal movement allocation

- **Causal proposal:** Redistributing an unchanged walking target into brief bouts after the three largest meals will improve 12-week physical energy and walking adherence in sedentary adults with dysglycemia more than completing the same target at unrestricted times.
- **Generated from:** [[daily-movement-mobility-and-pain]], [[free-sugars-and-glycemic-response]], and [[proactive-health-monitoring]].
- **Why it might matter:** Acute crossover trials support timing-specific glycemic effects, but a useful intervention must improve symptoms, function, or adherence rather than only a glucose trace.
- **Strongest alternative:** Any benefit comes from more walking, monitoring, or expectancy; meal timing does not change lived outcomes.
- **Discriminating observation:** With accelerometer-verified steps matched, post-meal allocation should improve a fatigue/energy score and post-meal sleepiness; glucose change without a human outcome would reject promotion.
- **Primary safety concern:** Falls, angina, hypoglycemia with glucose-lowering treatment, or gastrointestinal discomfort require clinical exclusions and screening.
- **Evidence review (2026-08-23):** Acute evidence supports the timing-to-glucose edge but not the proposed lived-outcome edge. A meta-analysis of eight small, high-risk-of-bias crossover trials (116 participants) found lower acute postprandial glucose after post-meal exercise than before-meal exercise or inactivity, while a broader 2024 meta-analysis found no significant glycemic advantage for post- versus pre-meal activity in the multiweek trials and rated the evidence low to moderate.[Engeroff et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36715875/) [Slebe et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38548105/) The closest allocation trial randomized 41 adults with type 2 diabetes in a two-week crossover and found 12% lower postprandial glucose iAUC with three 10-minute post-meal walks than one unrestricted 30-minute daily walk, but it did not test fatigue, function, quality of life, or durable adherence.[Reynolds et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27747394/) A 120-day crossover comparing three 15-minute post-meal walks with one 45-minute morning walk reported improved glucose profiles and HbA1c, but lacked washout and carbohydrate control and still did not establish a human functional benefit.[Pahra et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28854935/) This preserves the seed as an implementation question rather than treating glucose lowering as benefit.
- **Evidence update (2026-08-30):** Two newer syntheses strengthen the acute biomarker edge without closing the lived-outcome gap. A meta-analysis of 17 randomized and non-randomized crossover studies in adults with type 2 diabetes reported modest reductions in 24-hour mean glucose, time above range, glycemic variability, and breakfast iAUC after acute postprandial exercise; its apparently larger effects after breakfast or when exercise began more than 60 minutes after eating were not supported by significant between-timing subgroup differences, so they do not justify narrowing the seed to a particular meal or delay.[Lazić and Trajković, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42474912/) A second meta-analysis pooled 29 independent acute cohorts (573 participants) and found lower postprandial glucose and insulin AUC with accumulated bouts of 10 minutes or less versus continuous sitting, but no triglyceride effect; the authors characterized subgroup findings as exploratory and stated that long-term effects and real-world generalizability remain unknown.[Gong et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42586449/) Neither synthesis tested equal-step post-meal allocation against unrestricted walking for 12-week energy, somnolence, physical function, quality of life, or durable adherence. The causal claim, rank, evidence-distance score, and promotion deferral therefore remain unchanged.
- **Research before promotion:** Locate or design an at-least-12-week, free-living, equal-step comparison that measures a validated fatigue, vitality, post-meal somnolence, physical-function, or quality-of-life primary endpoint; prespecify accelerometer-confirmed dose equivalence, adherence, medication strata, hypoglycemia surveillance, and mobility exclusions. Evidence that timing changes glucose alone is insufficient.
- **Scores:** leverage 5 · novelty 3 · testability 5 · evidence distance 4 · safety 4

### 2. Minimum viable healthspan portfolio

- **Causal proposal:** Beginning with one deliberately small action in each of resistance training, aerobic activity, sleep regularity, and food quality, then expanding only after adherence stabilizes, will produce greater one-year functional improvement than optimizing one domain at a time or prescribing a comprehensive plan immediately.
- **Generated from:** [[practice-playbook]], [[mental-strength-and-behavioral-skills]], [[exercise-program-design]], [[sleep-quality-and-circadian-alignment]], and [[nutrition-evidence-and-personalization]].
- **Why it might matter:** Multiple modest actions may cover more causal pathways, but complexity can destroy adherence; sequencing breadth before dose could find a better balance.
- **Strongest alternative:** Results reflect coaching contact or selection of easier behaviors, and concentrating on the highest-risk domain yields larger benefits than spreading effort.
- **Discriminating observation:** An attention-matched pragmatic trial should show higher maintained cross-domain adherence plus better function or quality of life—not merely more checked boxes—than comprehensive and single-domain strategies.
- **Primary safety concern:** A generic minimum could delay appropriately intensive treatment or rehabilitation in people with substantial clinical risk.
- **Research before promotion:** Review factorial and multiple-behavior intervention trials; define minimum doses, escalation criteria, clinical exclusions, adherence, function, and quality-of-life outcomes.
- **Scores:** leverage 5 · novelty 4 · testability 4 · evidence distance 3 · safety 4

### 3. Question-first gate for optional preventive testing

- **Causal proposal:** Requiring adults considering optional preventive testing to state the functional, symptomatic, or risk decision they want the result to change before selecting any test will reduce six-month low-value testing and unresolved health concern without reducing completion of guideline-indicated screening compared with a biomarker-menu consultation of equal duration.
- **Generated from:** [[proactive-health-monitoring]] and [[mental-strength-and-behavioral-skills]].
- **Why it might matter:** Optional testing often begins with an available measurement rather than a clinical question; an upstream decision gate could redirect care toward the actual concern while preserving tests that can change management.
- **Strongest alternative:** The structured question merely suppresses patient choice or shifts testing outside the study, while clinician attention, baseline anxiety, or insurance access—not the decision gate—determines utilization and concern resolution.
- **Discriminating observation:** With clinician time, access, and written safety-netting matched, the question-first arm should produce fewer tests lacking a prespecified management consequence and better resolution of the presenting concern without lower completion of guideline-indicated screening or follow-up; lower test counts alone would fail promotion.
- **Primary safety concern:** A rigid gate could dismiss poorly articulated symptoms, amplify access inequities, or delay evaluation of a meaningful abnormality; new symptoms, red flags, high-risk history, and clinician judgment must override it.
- **Research before promotion:** Review diagnostic-stewardship, shared-decision-making, and pretest-counseling studies; define optional versus indicated testing, a reproducible decision-question prompt, red-flag overrides, concern-resolution and anxiety measures, outside-system testing capture, and blinded adjudication of appropriate care.
- **Scores:** leverage 5 · novelty 4 · testability 4 · evidence distance 3 · safety 4

### 4. Personalized meal-pattern testing by satiety and adherence

- **Causal proposal:** A crossover comparing two nutritionally adequate meal structures for each person, selected using hunger, spontaneous intake, gastrointestinal tolerance, and adherence rather than glucose response alone, will produce better six-month weight maintenance and dietary quality than generic meal-pattern advice.
- **Generated from:** [[satiety-oriented-diet-design]], [[nutrition-evidence-and-personalization]], [[free-sugars-and-glycemic-response]], and [[food-patterns-and-gut-ecology]].
- **Why it might matter:** People vary in satiety and tolerance, while short-term glucose responses can dominate personalization despite uncertain independent clinical value.
- **Strongest alternative:** Apparent personalization effects are novelty, preference, or measurement noise; ordinary high-quality dietary counseling performs equally well with less burden.
- **Discriminating observation:** Participants assigned to the pattern favored by prespecified satiety and tolerance criteria should maintain adherence and weight or waist outcomes better than those assigned by glucose response or generic advice.
- **Primary safety concern:** Intensive tracking may worsen anxiety or disordered eating and may be inappropriate for diabetes, gastrointestinal disease, pregnancy, or medically prescribed diets without clinical supervision.
- **Research before promotion:** Review human personalized-nutrition and N-of-1 feeding studies; define low-burden measurements, washout, diet-quality constraints, exclusion criteria, and clinically meaningful outcomes.
- **Scores:** leverage 4 · novelty 3 · testability 4 · evidence distance 2 · safety 3

### 5. Add-before-subtract dietary counseling

- **Causal proposal:** Adding one prespecified fiber- or protein-rich food to a recurrent low-quality eating occasion before asking participants to remove foods will improve six-month diet quality and retention more than restriction-first counseling with matched contact.
- **Generated from:** [[nutrition-evidence-and-personalization]], [[satiety-oriented-diet-design]], and [[dietary-fiber]].
- **Why it might matter:** An additive first step may reduce reactance and make a beneficial substitution easier to sustain.
- **Strongest alternative:** Added foods raise energy intake, and any retention advantage reflects a more pleasant message rather than a causal sequence.
- **Discriminating observation:** Diet quality should improve without higher energy intake or weight and persist after both arms receive the same removal goal.
- **Primary safety concern:** Energy excess, gastrointestinal symptoms, allergy, kidney-disease constraints, or disordered-eating preoccupation.
- **Research before promotion:** Find direct sequencing trials; define additions, substitution targets, exclusions, diet quality, retention, energy, weight, and gastrointestinal endpoints.
- **Scores:** leverage 4 · novelty 4 · testability 5 · evidence distance 3 · safety 4

### 6. Function-gated mobility drill selection

- **Causal proposal:** Retaining a low-risk mobility or corrective drill only when it produces a prespecified immediate improvement in a relevant standardized task will improve 12-week physical function and reduce time spent on ineffective drills more than selecting drills from symptoms and anatomy alone in adults with recurrent non-red-flag movement limitation.
- **Generated from:** [[daily-movement-mobility-and-pain]] and [[exercise-program-design]].
- **Why it might matter:** Immediate test–retest is accessible and individualized, but its practical value depends on whether a short-lived response predicts durable function rather than merely feeling persuasive in the moment.
- **Strongest alternative:** Immediate gains are warm-up, learning, regression, or expectancy effects; ordinary symptom-guided progressive exercise produces equal or better long-term function without repeated screening.
- **Discriminating observation:** With total exercise time, progression, contact, and retesting burden matched, the function-gated arm should improve a prespecified real-world task and discontinue more nonresponding drills; transient range or comfort without superior 12-week function would fail promotion.
- **Primary safety concern:** A reassuring short-term response could delay diagnosis or encourage repeated provocative testing; trauma, progressive weakness, neurological signs, systemic symptoms, severe balance impairment, and other red flags require exclusion or referral.
- **Research before promotion:** Review prognostic test–retest and matched-exercise studies; define reliable task thresholds, sham or alternate-drill controls, progression rules, red-flag screening, function and participation endpoints, adherence, adverse events, and a stopping rule for symptom worsening.
- **Scores:** leverage 4 · novelty 4 · testability 5 · evidence distance 3 · safety 4

### 7. Morning-light anchor before bedtime optimization

- **Causal proposal:** Establishing a consistent wake time plus outdoor morning light before adding evening sleep-hygiene changes will improve eight-week daytime function and sleep regularity more than beginning with an equally intensive evening routine in adults with irregular sleep but no untreated sleep disorder.
- **Generated from:** [[sleep-quality-and-circadian-alignment]] and [[pre-sleep-routines-and-stimulus-control]].
- **Why it might matter:** A phase-setting anchor may make later changes easier, while bedtime packages confound several components.
- **Strongest alternative:** Extra outdoor time, expectancy, adequate sleep opportunity, or evening arousal—not morning phase anchoring—drives benefit.
- **Discriminating observation:** Morning-first delivery should improve daytime function and sleep-midpoint variability before the common evening component begins; regularity without function would fail promotion.
- **Primary safety concern:** Mania risk, migraine, photosensitivity, eye disease, or worsened sleep deprivation from a rigid wake time.
- **Research before promotion:** Review light and sequencing trials; define apnea, bipolar and eye-disease screening, safe exposure, and a daytime-function primary endpoint.
- **Scores:** leverage 4 · novelty 3 · testability 4 · evidence distance 3 · safety 3

### 8. Response-adaptive movement-break timing

- **Causal proposal:** Allocating a fixed daily movement-break budget when a brief standardized function check first deteriorates will improve 12-week workday function and musculoskeletal comfort more than distributing the same break time at fixed intervals in desk-based adults with recurrent non-red-flag discomfort.
- **Generated from:** [[daily-movement-mobility-and-pain]] and [[exercise-program-design]].
- **Why it might matter:** Fixed reminders ignore when prolonged positioning begins to affect an individual, while response-adaptive timing could preserve function without increasing total intervention time or notification burden.
- **Strongest alternative:** Self-checks merely increase symptom attention or expectancy, and regular fixed breaks work equally well or better because they prevent deterioration before it is noticed.
- **Discriminating observation:** With total break minutes, prompts, and movement options matched, the response-adaptive arm should improve repeated task function and end-of-day interference without increasing symptom vigilance; comfort alone without functional improvement would fail promotion.
- **Primary safety concern:** Repeated self-testing could amplify pain vigilance, while movement prompts could be unsafe for people with acute injury, neurological signs, severe balance limitation, or work tasks that cannot be interrupted safely.
- **Research before promotion:** Review just-in-time adaptive activity interventions and movement-break trials; select a low-burden function check, define red-flag exclusions, cap testing frequency, and prespecify adherence, symptom-vigilance, work-function, and adverse-event outcomes.
- **Scores:** leverage 4 · novelty 4 · testability 4 · evidence distance 3 · safety 4

### 9. Uncertainty-framed preventive monitoring

- **Causal proposal:** Presenting routine preventive results with expected within-person variation, an explicit action threshold, and a scheduled confirmation plan will reduce six-month health anxiety and unnecessary follow-up while preserving completion of indicated care compared with displaying the same results in a trend dashboard without uncertainty framing.
- **Generated from:** [[proactive-health-monitoring]] and [[mental-strength-and-behavioral-skills]].
- **Why it might matter:** Longitudinal monitoring can support decisions, but unexplained fluctuation and incidental abnormalities may generate anxiety, repeated testing, and low-value care that erase its practical benefit.
- **Strongest alternative:** Anxiety and follow-up are driven by the result itself or baseline health anxiety; explanatory framing either has no effect or falsely reassures people and reduces appropriate care.
- **Discriminating observation:** With measurements, clinician access, and contact matched, uncertainty framing should lower validated health-anxiety scores and non-indicated repeat testing without reducing prespecified indicated confirmations, referrals, or treatment adherence.
- **Primary safety concern:** Poorly calibrated reassurance could delay evaluation of meaningful abnormalities or symptoms, especially when action thresholds do not fit individual clinical risk.
- **Research before promotion:** Review risk-communication, patient-portal, incidental-result, and test-repetition studies; define guideline-linked action categories, clinician override, high-risk exclusions, and adjudication of appropriate versus unnecessary follow-up.
- **Scores:** leverage 4 · novelty 4 · testability 4 · evidence distance 3 · safety 3

### 10. Replication-gated testing of suspected food intolerance

- **Causal proposal:** Requiring a suspected non-allergic food–symptom relationship to recur during two brief, nutritionally adequate withdrawal-and-rechallenge cycles before longer avoidance will preserve dietary variety and reduce symptom-related disruption over 12 weeks more than acting on a single unblinded food challenge in adults with intermittent non-red-flag gastrointestinal symptoms.
- **Generated from:** [[nutrition-evidence-and-personalization]] and [[mental-strength-and-behavioral-skills]].
- **Why it might matter:** Ordinary symptom fluctuation can make one exposure look causal, leading to unnecessary restriction; replication could separate repeatable responses from noise while retaining a feasible human N-of-1 design.
- **Strongest alternative:** Repeated challenges increase vigilance and burden, symptoms vary with dose or context too much to replicate, and dietitian-guided usual care preserves variety equally well without formal cycles.
- **Discriminating observation:** With dietitian contact, symptom logging, and challenge foods matched, the replication-gated arm should maintain more prespecified food groups with no worse symptom interference; fewer exclusions caused only by ignoring reproducible symptoms, or symptom improvement caused by broader restriction, would fail promotion.
- **Primary safety concern:** Rechallenge is inappropriate for suspected allergy, celiac disease before diagnostic testing, severe reactions, significant weight loss, bleeding, inflammatory disease, pregnancy-related constraints, eating disorders, or medically required diets.
- **Research before promotion:** Review blinded and unblinded N-of-1 food-challenge methods; define eligible symptom patterns, standardized dose and context, washout and replication criteria, diet-quality floor, diagnostic and red-flag exclusions, dietary-variety and symptom-interference endpoints, and stopping rules.
- **Scores:** leverage 4 · novelty 4 · testability 4 · evidence distance 3 · safety 3

### 11. Chronotype-aware sleep-regularity window

- **Causal proposal:** Anchoring sleep within a personalized timing window derived from baseline chronotype and obligations will improve 12-week daytime function and schedule adherence more than prescribing the same narrow clock-time target to all adults with irregular sleep and no untreated sleep disorder.
- **Generated from:** [[sleep-quality-and-circadian-alignment]] and [[mental-strength-and-behavioral-skills]].
- **Why it might matter:** Regularity may be easier to sustain when the target respects biological timing and real constraints, but personalization could also legitimize schedules that remain misaligned with light exposure or social demands.
- **Strongest alternative:** Adequate sleep opportunity and consistent wake time explain any benefit; chronotype-based windows add complexity without improving function or adherence.
- **Discriminating observation:** Against an attention-matched fixed-window arm with equal sleep opportunity, the personalized arm should improve a validated daytime-function outcome and maintained timing adherence; actigraphic regularity alone would fail promotion.
- **Primary safety concern:** A timing intervention could worsen sleep restriction, drowsy driving, mood instability, or conflict with shift-work and caregiving demands; suspected apnea, bipolar-spectrum illness, and hazardous sleepiness require screening or clinical management.
- **Research before promotion:** Review chronotype-matched scheduling and sleep-regularity trials; define the baseline assessment, allowable window, minimum sleep opportunity, safety exclusions, adherence measure, and daytime-function primary endpoint.
- **Scores:** leverage 4 · novelty 3 · testability 4 · evidence distance 3 · safety 3

### 12. Constraint-first exercise troubleshooting

- **Causal proposal:** Identifying and changing one prespecified binding constraint—schedule instability, excessive session duration, movement intolerance, or inadequate recovery opportunity—before revising exercise dose will improve 16-week training adherence and physical function more than adding generic motivational coaching to the unchanged program in insufficiently active adults whose initial plan is not being completed.
- **Generated from:** [[exercise-program-design]] and [[mental-strength-and-behavioral-skills]].
- **Why it might matter:** Exercise plans often fail upstream of physiology; a constraint-first diagnostic could preserve an otherwise adequate program while avoiding the burden and confusion of changing several variables at once.
- **Strongest alternative:** The apparent bottleneck is unstable or misidentified, and benefit comes from extra attention, easier expectations, or regression after an unusually poor adherence period rather than constraint-specific repair.
- **Discriminating observation:** With contact time, encouragement, and exercise prescription matched, the constraint-first arm should complete more planned sessions and improve a prespecified physical-function outcome; adherence without functional improvement, or equal improvement after generic coaching, would fail promotion.
- **Primary safety concern:** Treating pain, severe fatigue, cardiopulmonary symptoms, low energy availability, or mood disorder as an adherence bottleneck could delay clinical assessment; red flags and exercise contraindications require screening and referral.
- **Research before promotion:** Review adherence-barrier tailoring and stepped exercise interventions; define a reproducible constraint-classification rule, contact-matched comparator, minimum effective exercise dose, clinical exclusions, adherence verification, function endpoint, and criteria for revising a misclassified constraint.
- **Scores:** leverage 4 · novelty 3 · testability 4 · evidence distance 3 · safety 3

## Seed format

Each seed records a causal proposal, source wiki gaps, potential human value, strongest alternative, discriminating observation, safety concern, research needed before promotion, and scores for leverage, novelty, testability, evidence distance, and safety.
