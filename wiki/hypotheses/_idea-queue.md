# Hypothesis idea nursery

This bounded queue contains creative but unreviewed human-direct ideas. Entries are research prompts, not conclusions, advice, or invitations to self-experiment. The weekly workshop generates three seeds unless twelve are active, then may promote at most one after scholarly review. Scores run from 1 (low) to 5 (high); higher evidence distance means more missing inferential steps.

## Active seeds

### 1. Post-meal movement allocation

- **Causal proposal:** Redistributing an unchanged walking target into brief bouts after the three largest meals will improve 12-week physical energy and walking adherence in sedentary adults with dysglycemia more than completing the same target at unrestricted times.
- **Generated from:** [[daily-movement-mobility-and-pain]], [[free-sugars-and-glycemic-response]], and [[proactive-health-monitoring]].
- **Why it might matter:** Acute crossover trials support timing-specific glycemic effects, but a useful intervention must improve symptoms, function, or adherence rather than only a glucose trace.
- **Strongest alternative:** Any benefit comes from more walking, monitoring, or expectancy; meal timing does not change lived outcomes.
- **Discriminating observation:** With accelerometer-verified steps matched, post-meal allocation should improve a fatigue/energy score and post-meal sleepiness; glucose change without a human outcome would reject promotion.
- **Primary safety concern:** Falls, angina, hypoglycemia with glucose-lowering treatment, or gastrointestinal discomfort require clinical exclusions and screening.
- **Research before promotion:** Review longer free-living timing trials, validate symptom endpoints, define medication and mobility exclusions, and establish equal-step adherence.
- **Scores:** leverage 5 · novelty 3 · testability 5 · evidence distance 2 · safety 4

### 2. Minimum viable healthspan portfolio

- **Causal proposal:** Beginning with one deliberately small action in each of resistance training, aerobic activity, sleep regularity, and food quality, then expanding only after adherence stabilizes, will produce greater one-year functional improvement than optimizing one domain at a time or prescribing a comprehensive plan immediately.
- **Generated from:** [[practice-playbook]], [[mental-strength-and-behavioral-skills]], [[exercise-program-design]], [[sleep-quality-and-circadian-alignment]], and [[nutrition-evidence-and-personalization]].
- **Why it might matter:** Multiple modest actions may cover more causal pathways, but complexity can destroy adherence; sequencing breadth before dose could find a better balance.
- **Strongest alternative:** Results reflect coaching contact or selection of easier behaviors, and concentrating on the highest-risk domain yields larger benefits than spreading effort.
- **Discriminating observation:** An attention-matched pragmatic trial should show higher maintained cross-domain adherence plus better function or quality of life—not merely more checked boxes—than comprehensive and single-domain strategies.
- **Primary safety concern:** A generic minimum could delay appropriately intensive treatment or rehabilitation in people with substantial clinical risk.
- **Research before promotion:** Review factorial and multiple-behavior intervention trials; define minimum doses, escalation criteria, clinical exclusions, adherence, function, and quality-of-life outcomes.
- **Scores:** leverage 5 · novelty 4 · testability 4 · evidence distance 3 · safety 4

### 3. Personalized meal-pattern testing by satiety and adherence

- **Causal proposal:** A crossover comparing two nutritionally adequate meal structures for each person, selected using hunger, spontaneous intake, gastrointestinal tolerance, and adherence rather than glucose response alone, will produce better six-month weight maintenance and dietary quality than generic meal-pattern advice.
- **Generated from:** [[satiety-oriented-diet-design]], [[nutrition-evidence-and-personalization]], [[free-sugars-and-glycemic-response]], and [[food-patterns-and-gut-ecology]].
- **Why it might matter:** People vary in satiety and tolerance, while short-term glucose responses can dominate personalization despite uncertain independent clinical value.
- **Strongest alternative:** Apparent personalization effects are novelty, preference, or measurement noise; ordinary high-quality dietary counseling performs equally well with less burden.
- **Discriminating observation:** Participants assigned to the pattern favored by prespecified satiety and tolerance criteria should maintain adherence and weight or waist outcomes better than those assigned by glucose response or generic advice.
- **Primary safety concern:** Intensive tracking may worsen anxiety or disordered eating and may be inappropriate for diabetes, gastrointestinal disease, pregnancy, or medically prescribed diets without clinical supervision.
- **Research before promotion:** Review human personalized-nutrition and N-of-1 feeding studies; define low-burden measurements, washout, diet-quality constraints, exclusion criteria, and clinically meaningful outcomes.
- **Scores:** leverage 4 · novelty 3 · testability 4 · evidence distance 2 · safety 3

### 4. Add-before-subtract dietary counseling

- **Causal proposal:** Adding one prespecified fiber- or protein-rich food to a recurrent low-quality eating occasion before asking participants to remove foods will improve six-month diet quality and retention more than restriction-first counseling with matched contact.
- **Generated from:** [[nutrition-evidence-and-personalization]], [[satiety-oriented-diet-design]], and [[dietary-fiber]].
- **Why it might matter:** An additive first step may reduce reactance and make a beneficial substitution easier to sustain.
- **Strongest alternative:** Added foods raise energy intake, and any retention advantage reflects a more pleasant message rather than a causal sequence.
- **Discriminating observation:** Diet quality should improve without higher energy intake or weight and persist after both arms receive the same removal goal.
- **Primary safety concern:** Energy excess, gastrointestinal symptoms, allergy, kidney-disease constraints, or disordered-eating preoccupation.
- **Research before promotion:** Find direct sequencing trials; define additions, substitution targets, exclusions, diet quality, retention, energy, weight, and gastrointestinal endpoints.
- **Scores:** leverage 4 · novelty 4 · testability 5 · evidence distance 3 · safety 4

### 5. Morning-light anchor before bedtime optimization

- **Causal proposal:** Establishing a consistent wake time plus outdoor morning light before adding evening sleep-hygiene changes will improve eight-week daytime function and sleep regularity more than beginning with an equally intensive evening routine in adults with irregular sleep but no untreated sleep disorder.
- **Generated from:** [[sleep-quality-and-circadian-alignment]] and [[pre-sleep-routines-and-stimulus-control]].
- **Why it might matter:** A phase-setting anchor may make later changes easier, while bedtime packages confound several components.
- **Strongest alternative:** Extra outdoor time, expectancy, adequate sleep opportunity, or evening arousal—not morning phase anchoring—drives benefit.
- **Discriminating observation:** Morning-first delivery should improve daytime function and sleep-midpoint variability before the common evening component begins; regularity without function would fail promotion.
- **Primary safety concern:** Mania risk, migraine, photosensitivity, eye disease, or worsened sleep deprivation from a rigid wake time.
- **Research before promotion:** Review light and sequencing trials; define apnea, bipolar and eye-disease screening, safe exposure, and a daytime-function primary endpoint.
- **Scores:** leverage 4 · novelty 3 · testability 4 · evidence distance 3 · safety 3

## Seed format

Each seed records a causal proposal, source wiki gaps, potential human value, strongest alternative, discriminating observation, safety concern, research needed before promotion, and scores for leverage, novelty, testability, evidence distance, and safety.
