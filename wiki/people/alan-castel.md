# Alan Castel

Alan Castel is a UCLA cognitive psychologist who studies human memory and cognitive aging. His teaching centers on metacognition: people need an accurate model of what they know, what they merely recognize, and which failures matter before they can allocate attention and practice well. (@hubermanlab (Andrew Huberman) — "How to Improve Your Memory & Cognitive Function at Any Age | Dr. Alan Castel", 2026-07-13, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EIhilBpn8Ow))

## Evidence stance and distinctive positions

Castel treats memory as selective reconstruction rather than a recording capacity to maximize. His most useful learning sequence is attempt, error, feedback, and corrected retrieval: passive familiarity can coexist with poor recall, while an unsuccessful attempt can make the later correction more learnable. He also distinguishes arbitrary-label memory from conceptual understanding and everyday function; forgetting a name is not equivalent to forgetting whether a person is trustworthy, how a medicine affected you, or where an emergency exit is. These distinctions are developed at [[memory-encoding-retrieval-and-reconstruction]]. (@hubermanlab (Andrew Huberman) — "How to Improve Your Memory & Cognitive Function at Any Age | Dr. Alan Castel", 2026-07-13, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EIhilBpn8Ow))

His distinctive aging position is selection-first rather than decline-only. Older adults may lose speed or source detail while becoming better at preserving schema-consistent, curiosity-relevant, and emotionally meaningful information; successful adaptation can therefore mean selective optimization with compensation rather than reproducing young-adult performance in every domain. His ABC framing—attitude and adaptation, balance, and connection—is a synthesis of successful-aging observations, not a validated protocol, and his claims about positive age beliefs, purpose, grandparent contact, and superagers remain partly correlational. ## Practical implications

- Use Castel's work to design learning around active retrieval, corrective feedback, and value-based selection, while looking to systematic reviews rather than interview confidence for the evidence grade. ## Gaps & open questions

- Which of Castel's state-curiosity and age-selectivity findings replicate outside trivia tasks and selected older-adult samples?
- How much of the successful-aging framing is causal, and how much reflects health, wealth, education, survivorship, and access to meaningful roles?
