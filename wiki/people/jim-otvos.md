# Jim Otvos

Physical chemist and inventor of clinical NMR lipoprotein testing (the
NMR LipoProfile / LDL-P test). PhD in chemistry and biochemistry; ~20
years in academia using NMR spectroscopy as a structural tool, with
appointments at the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee and, from 1990,
North Carolina State University (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood
analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single blood
sample", 2026-08-03, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## Career arc

- Entered lipoprotein work by investigating a 1986 New England Journal of
  Medicine paper claiming a simple NMR line-width test could detect
  cancer; showed the "cancer signal" was really a lipoprotein signal
  (narrowing driven by high triglycerides plus low HDL cholesterol) and
  published the refutation in Clinical Chemistry around 1990–91 (Peter
  Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be
  assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- Realized the same composite signal could be deconvolved to quantify
  VLDL, LDL, and HDL — and later their size subclasses — from a single
  30-second NMR scan; validated small-vs-large LDL detection against Ron
  Krauss's gradient gel electrophoresis samples (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒
  NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a
  single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- Founded LipoScience in the mid-1990s to translate NMR testing into
  clinical laboratories; obtained FDA clearance for LDL-P and the Vantera
  NMR analyzer in 2011. LipoScience went public and was acquired by
  LabCorp in 2014, ending the in-vitro-diagnostics vision of placing NMR
  analyzers in laboratories worldwide (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood
  analysis: how mortality risk and more can be assessed from a single
  blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- Continued biomarker R&D inside LabCorp — [[lpir-score]], the diabetes
  risk index (DRI), [[glyca]], and [[mvx]] — before leaving to spend his
  remaining years on the science rather than fighting US reimbursement
  economics (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality
  risk and more can be assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).

## Positions

- LDL particle number (LDL-P), not particle size, drives LDL-associated
  risk; "large fluffy LDL is benign" is, in his words, a fallacious idea
  (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more
  can be assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)). See
  [[ldl-particle-number]] and [[ldl-particle-size]].
- Partners with ApoB advocate Allan Sniderman rather than competing:
  LDL-P and ApoB tell the same story against cholesterol-only measurement
  (Peter Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more
  can be assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
- Warns that the aging first-generation Vantera analyzers are the only
  clinical NMR analyzers in the world; unless an IVD company licenses the
  technology from LabCorp, this testing capability could be lost (Peter
  Attia MD — "402 ‒ NMR blood analysis: how mortality risk and more can be
  assessed from a single blood sample", 2026-08-03,
  [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IMbghqZ1iXI)).
