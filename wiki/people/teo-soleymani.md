# Teo Soleymani

Teo Soleymani is a board-certified dermatologist and fellowship-trained skin cancer and reconstructive surgeon (formerly UCLA faculty, now in private practice in Pasadena) whose public teaching centers on skin cancer, sun exposure, and skin-health fundamentals. He is the author of the lay book *Skin Protocols* and co-founder of the supplement Sunpowder — a commercial conflict relevant to his supplement and astaxanthin claims. His self-description as the anti-dermatologist dermatologist marks his contrarian stance toward his own specialty's sun messaging. (@maxlugavere (Max Lugavere) — "The Skin Doctor: Hair Loss, Itchy Skin, Sunscreen Myths, and Anti-Aging Truths", 2026-07-15, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99LCstvkNVw))

## Positions and distinctive perspectives

- **Safe sun exposure over sun avoidance.** He argues dermatology's blanket sun-fear is partly industry-driven (a beauty industry approaching $700 billion that needs sunscreen fear to sell product — his causal attribution, not established fact), that complete avoidance carries its own costs, and that exposure should be personalized to one's minimal erythemal dose. He distinguishes sharply between the sun–photoaging relationship (linear, predictable) and the sun–cancer relationship (looser, immune-modified). ([[skin-cancer-risk-and-immune-surveillance]], [[photoprotection]])
- **Immune surveillance as the undervalued skin-cancer variable.** Transplant-level immunosuppression multiplies skin-cancer risk regardless of sun behavior; he frames immune and metabolic health as legitimate, neglected levers alongside photoprotection.
- **Anti-guilt clinical ethic.** He rejects telling patients their cancer is their fault, and supports this with a contested mechanistic claim — that 28-day epidermal turnover prevents decades-old sunburn mutations from persisting. The mainstream stem-cell persistence model contradicts the mechanism; the disagreement is recorded at [[skin-cancer-risk-and-immune-surveillance]].
- **Minimalist routine.** Three fundamentals — some form of sun protection, a nighttime retinoid, and a balanced lifestyle (sleep, anti-inflammatory diet) — are, in his framing, about 90% of achievable skin health; serums and adjuncts are icing. He calls the retinoid the only anti-aging medicine proven over 50 years and says it should be in the water. ([[topical-retinoids]], [[skincare-evidence-and-routine-design]])
- **Mineral-sunscreen preference, physical-protection-first.** He personally favors hats, shade, and clothing over sunscreen (impossible to burn in the shade), prefers mineral filters when using sunscreen, and emphasizes that most users under-apply and miss areas. ([[mineral-and-organic-sunscreens]], [[photoprotection]])
- **Skin as metabolic readout.** Acanthosis nigricans, rapidly multiplying skin tags, and flaring inflammatory disease are read as insulin-resistance and diet signals; psoriasis is a systemic metabolic-immune marker improvable by weight loss and diet. ([[cutaneous-signs-of-systemic-disease]], [[psoriasis-and-systemic-inflammation]], [[diet-and-inflammatory-skin-disease]])
- **Hair-loss pharmacology with consent emphasis.** He favors oral minoxidil over topicals, reports real-world sexual side-effect rates for finasteride far above the package insert (~50% versus 2–3% — a practice observation conflicting with trial data), frames much post-finasteride syndrome as nocebo while insisting side-effect disclosure is non-negotiable, and criticizes online pharmacies for consent-free prescribing. ([[hair-loss-diagnosis-and-scalp-health]])
- **Commercially conflicted supplement advocacy.** He promotes astaxanthin (an ingredient of his own Sunpowder) for skin photoprotection and claims emerging cancer-reduction signals; these claims should be read against the conflict and the absence of independent outcome trials. ([[photoprotection]], [[supplement-evidence-and-safety]])
