# David Rabin

David Rabin is a board-certified psychiatrist and neuroscientist (MD, PhD; PTSD research at the University of Pittsburgh Medical Center), author of *A Simple Guide to Being Alive*, a trainer in ketamine-assisted therapy, and co-founder of Apollo Neuroscience — a commercial conflict that colors his wearable and vibration claims. His unifying framework is that felt, physiological safety is the master variable of mental health: safety gates learning and memory, its absence (chronic threat appraisal) drives most common pathology, and the fastest levers on it are bottom-up sensory signals — soothing touch, familiar music, slow voluntary breathing. (@maxlugavere (Max Lugavere) — "'You Are Not Your Thoughts!' How to Stop Negative Thoughts Instantly", 2026-07-08, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vEY4oGH1x_0))

## Positions and distinctive perspectives

- **Anxiety as neutral signal, not disorder.** Anxiety evolved as an attention-allocation signal from the amygdala and is never supposed to disappear; feelings fully attended to resolve in roughly 60–120 seconds, while suppression amplifies them. The time constant is his teaching heuristic, not a measured universal. ([[emotion-regulation]])
- **Stress tolerance as trained skill.** Resilience is learned self-soothing plus graded stress exposure (hormesis) beginning in early childhood; differences between adults reflect mostly training history, with a component he attributes to transgenerational epigenetic inheritance of trauma and resilience — a real but early literature he states with more confidence than it currently supports. ([[stress-threat-discrimination]], [[mental-strength-and-behavioral-skills]])
- **Safety as a prerequisite for learning.** Durable understanding requires a vagal, safety-dominant state; fight-or-flight physiology blocks encoding. His insula-before-amygdala sameness-first framing is expert simplification of real circuitry.
- **Information overload as ambient terror.** A half hour of morning screen use delivers, in his estimate, the informational input of a 1950s week; sustained overload keeps the amygdala firing a low-grade fear response he calls ambient terror, and he treats attention control as a trainable muscle (mindfulness as its bicep curl), with do-not-disturb as a foundational boundary. The 1950s comparison is illustrative, not a measured equivalence. ([[mental-strength-and-behavioral-skills]], [[meaning-boredom-and-technology]])
- **Psychedelic-assisted therapy as a class breakthrough.** He forecasts psychedelics will be remembered as psychiatry's antibiotics, cites 50–70% response/remission in MDMA PTSD trials against a status quo he characterizes as under 30% long-term response to standard care, positions ketamine as the legal, SSRI-compatible entry point, and insists therapy is about 80% of durable benefit — every psychiatric medication, in his account, works better with therapy. He co-ran an MDMA epigenetic study he says shows trauma-associated methylation moving toward pre-trauma patterns within three doses over 12 weeks; treat this as a preliminary, self-reported finding. His enthusiasm conflicts with more cautious clinical readings recorded at [[ketamine-and-psychedelic-therapy]].
- **Evidence-hierarchy contrarianism.** He argues Western medicine's you're-wrong-until-proven-right stance misreads how science works, citing antibiotics adopted from observation without placebo-controlled trials, and credits indigenous and Eastern traditions with working trauma knowledge. This observational-first stance trades false negatives for false positives; the wiki retains its standard hierarchy while recording the position.
- **Apollo wearable.** His company's vibro-tactile device claims to deliver safety-signal rhythms through touch receptors, with company-reported trials (a claimed ~46-minute average sleep gain in a large Oura-ring study, 17 trials overall). All of it is commercially conflicted and none was independently reviewed here.
