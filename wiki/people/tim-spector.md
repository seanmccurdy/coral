# Tim Spector

Tim Spector is a professor of genetic epidemiology at King's College London, co-founder of the nutrition company ZOE, and author of *Food for Life* and *Ferment*. His research base spans twin cohorts, the PREDICT postprandial-response studies, and ZOE's large self-reported community datasets; his public work centers on gut-microbiome diversity (the 30-plants-a-week heuristic), fermented foods, ultra-processed-food harm, and food-industry and policy critique. ## Evidence stance and distinctive positions

Spector's most contrarian scientific position is the unification of brain disorders: citing overlapping genetic risk, shared risk factors, and symptom convergence, he argues psychiatry's hundreds of symptom-defined diagnoses look "like it's just one disease that's just presenting differently at different times in life," with gut-microbiome abnormality and inflammation as the shared substrate — expert interpretation well ahead of consensus, recorded and bounded at [[nutritional-psychiatry]]. Related positions: the gut can be seen as directing the brain (he cites the vagus nerve as the body's biggest nerve with about 80% of traffic running gut-to-brain); microbiome improvement timelines are personalized but typically weeks-to-months, faster from a worse baseline; and subjective mood and energy may register dietary improvement before a microbiome test does. On policy he advocates banning medium/high-risk ultra-processed foods from public institutions, prohibiting health claims on unhealthy UPF packaging, and restoring food education in schools; he criticizes UK inaction relative to the dozen-plus countries whose guidelines now warn against UPFs, and he calls the NHS rollout of GLP-1 drugs without food-behavior support a squandered opportunity. On antimicrobial resistance he stakes out an early-adopter position on phage therapy — "pretty much the only approach to counteract the fact that we're running out of antibiotics" — ahead of the randomized evidence base ([[phage-therapy]]), and his twin-cohort work extends his diversity-equals-health reading from bacteria to gut phages ([[gut-virome-and-bacteriophages]]). ## Practical implications

- Read him as a credentialed epidemiologist whose public claims often run ahead of the underlying evidence tier: mechanisms and associations are frequently presented with causal confidence (mouse-transplant studies, prodromal-constipation epidemiology, "80% of traffic" nerve claims) that this wiki grades separately.
- Weigh commercial conflicts explicitly: he co-founded and promotes ZOE, whose products (personalized nutrition testing, Daily30, the Gut Health Bar) are marketed inside the same episodes that present the supporting science, and ZOE's own studies (PREDICT, Big IF, Daily30 RCT, the planned Gut Health Bar RCT) are company-run.

## Gaps & open questions

- How much of the ZOE community-study evidence (self-reported, non-blinded, customer-population) replicates in independent samples?
- Does his brain-disorder unification survive formal genetic and nosological testing?
