# Gayatri Devi

Gayatri Devi, M.D., is a New York neurologist and psychiatrist (board-certified in neurology, psychiatry, brain injury medicine, pain medicine, and behavioral neurology) subspecializing in memory disorders, in practice on the Upper East Side for 26 years and in the memory field since 1994. Her practice concentrates on high-functioning professionals who want to keep working, which shapes her methods: rigorous multi-hour testing to defeat compensation, domain-level rather than global staging, and treatment aimed at preserving function and agency. She compares the current moment to practicing infectious-disease medicine at the arrival of penicillin—dementia care now has disease-modifying options and personalizable drug cocktails. (Peter Attia MD — "399 - The evolution of Alzheimer's disease and dementia care | Gayatri Devi, M.D.", 2026-07-13, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x7NhqMOwdOM))

## Distinctive positions

- **Dementias are spectrum disorders, not binary diseases.** Presentation varies by person, region of pathology, and comorbidity, from mild non-progressive impairment to rapid decline; she analogizes to autism's spectrum. [[alzheimers-spectrum-and-diagnosis]] (Peter Attia MD — "399 - The evolution of Alzheimer's disease and dementia care | Gayatri Devi, M.D.", 2026-07-13, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x7NhqMOwdOM))
- **Alzheimer's patients can get better.** Her stated biggest change of mind: "I never thought that patients with Alzheimer's could get better. Ever." Biomarker-confirmed patients improving—sometimes with interventions as simple as brain exercises—forced the update against ingrained teaching of inexorable decline. (Peter Attia MD — "399 - The evolution of Alzheimer's disease and dementia care | Gayatri Devi, M.D.", 2026-07-13, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x7NhqMOwdOM))
- **Anti-staging.** She refuses single overall Alzheimer's stages, staging each functional domain separately instead; a global stage is in her words reductionist and a disservice to patient and family. (Peter Attia MD — "399 - The evolution of Alzheimer's disease and dementia care | Gayatri Devi, M.D.", 2026-07-13, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x7NhqMOwdOM))
- **Blood-biomarker skepticism (for now).** Self-described old school: blood tests are the future but are standardized against amyloid PET despite high asymptomatic amyloid prevalence; she confirms with amyloid-plus-tau imaging or CSF. [[alzheimers-diagnosis-biological-vs-clinical]]
- **Slow-titration anti-amyloid treatment, including APOE4/4 patients whom standard practice excludes.** An early aducanumab adopter, she reasons that decades-old amyloid need not be cleared fast; her published series reports ~4% ARIA with steroid premedication after ARIA and MRI-first surveillance. [[anti-amyloid-immunotherapy]]
- **Neuroinflammation as the coming frontier.** She predicts prevention will center on anti-inflammatory intervention beginning decades early, plus AI-based early detection of changes in thinking patterns and targeted neuromodulation for treatment; she has used neuronavigated TMS off-label since 2008.
- **Menopause-related cognitive impairment** is her coinage: objective, Alzheimer's-mimicking, treatable estrogen-deficiency impairment, including misdiagnosed dementia cases. [[menopause-related-cognitive-impairment]]
- **Lewy body prognosis optimism and misdiagnosis vigilance.** She rejects the five-to-six-year fatal framing, reports treatment response sometimes exceeding Alzheimer's, and campaigns against dopaminergic drugs mistakenly given to Lewy body patients. [[lewy-body-disease-and-synucleinopathies]]

## Practical implications

- Read her positions as expert practice patterns from a single specialized clinic—several (slow titration, TMS, treating 4/4 patients) are open-label and off-label, not trial-validated.
- Her would-I-take-this-drug-myself test and function-first framing are useful patient-side questions for any proposed dementia treatment.

## Gaps & open questions

- Which of her practice results (4% ARIA, IVIG plaque-negative cases, TMS language benefit) survive randomized replication?
- How generalizable are findings from a high-reserve, high-income professional patient population?
