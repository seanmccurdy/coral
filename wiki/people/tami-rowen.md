# Tami Rowen

Tami Rowen is an obstetrician-gynecologist specializing in women's sexual health. Her distinctive teaching move in the current corpus is holding two commitments simultaneously that most commentators trade off: she reads group-level trial data strictly (a mood benefit whose confidence interval crosses one "could just be due to chance"; body-composition trials of female testosterone "basically show it's neutral"), while insisting that neutral group averages must not be used to dismiss individual patients — her line is "just because I can't explain it physiologically, it doesn't mean it's not real". Her worked example is hormonal contraception: robust literature averages to a neutral sexual-health effect because subgroups who improve, worsen, and feel nothing cancel out, so the average is exactly the wrong tool for the patient in front of you. (@RenaMalikMD (Rena Malik, M.D.) — "She's Lubricated… But Does She Actually Want Sex? ft. Women's Sexual Health Expert", 2026-08-26, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ioXnBzREeis))

On physiology, her signature corrections target the menopause-testosterone narrative: female testosterone declines in the 30s, holds relatively low and steady through the 40s, and does not change through menopause — so "replacement because menopause depleted it" misstates the endocrinology even though treatment can still be reasonable for a defined indication. She rejects generalizations about women needing emotional connection for arousal ("there's too much variety and diversity of experiences to make any generalizations") and teaches arousal non-concordance — genital response and cognitive desire measured as separate, often uncorrelated channels — as foundational. She is explicit that women's sexual medicine runs decades behind men's on funding and data rigor, and positions herself against universal prescriptions in the menopause space in favor of informed individual choice. ## Practical implications

- Use her group-versus-individual framing when reading any hormone or contraception trial: a neutral average is compatible with heterogeneous individual responses. A temporal symptom relationship deserves assessment and shared consideration of alternatives, not automatic attribution or dismissal. (@RenaMalikMD (Rena Malik, M.D.) — "She's Lubricated… But Does She Actually Want Sex? ft. Women's Sexual Health Expert", 2026-08-26, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ioXnBzREeis))
- Treat her testosterone-physiology corrections as a filter for menopause marketing: a clinician proposing testosterone should name the indication and the expected evidence-backed benefit, not invoke menopausal depletion.
