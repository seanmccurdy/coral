# Lucy McBride

Lucy McBride is a primary-care internist in Washington, DC (trained at Harvard Medical School and Johns Hopkins), a Substack writer (her newsletter is called Are You Okay), and author of a 2026 book on patient self-advocacy with a foreword by Mary Claire Haver. Her project is a middle position between industrial medicine and wellness culture: medicine excels at fighting disease but fails at helping people live, and the wellness industry filled that vacuum — so the fix is neither dismissing wellness nor outsourcing to it, but teaching patients to ask better questions of themselves before asking anyone else for answers. (@maxlugavere (Max Lugavere) — "Wellness Myths Debunked: How to Navigate 'Optimization Slop' Culture", 2026-08-12, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z4A12iaH7I0))

## Evidence stance and distinctive perspectives

Her signature frameworks: the **question beneath the question** — patient requests (do I need a GLP-1?) usually encode an unexamined problem three or four questions down, and solving the stated question misses the real one; the **health ecosystem** — biometric data plus biographic data (relationships with food, alcohol, body, mortality), since normal labs cannot measure emotional distress, boundaries, or quality of life; and moving the **locus of control** inward — self-honesty first, then partnership with a clinician, explicitly not DIY medicine ([[proactive-health-monitoring]]). She treats certainty as the red flag of unreliable health communication (uncertainty is a feature of science, not a bug), extends that critique symmetrically to credentialed physicians holding forth outside their training, and names humility as medicine's underused repair tool for its trust deficit. Structurally she argues the US has a health-insurance system rather than a healthcare system, that scaling relationship-based primary care would save money and lives, and that AI belongs in the back office (prior authorizations, insurance haggling) to elevate rather than replace the patient–doctor relationship — policy positions from practice experience, not trial evidence. (@maxlugavere (Max Lugavere) — "Wellness Myths Debunked: How to Navigate 'Optimization Slop' Culture", 2026-08-12, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z4A12iaH7I0)) [[health-misinformation-and-media-incentives]]

Clinical positions recorded elsewhere: GLP-1 boundaries (no dose escalation to abolish normal hunger cues; disordered-eating vigilance; the on-drug window as habit-insight opportunity predicting successful weaning) at [[glp-1-receptor-agonists]]; the GLP-1-halo critique of unregulated peptides at [[experimental-peptides]]; and her stronger-than-guideline default-on hormone-therapy position with the WHI trust-injury narrative at [[whi-and-menopause-hormone-therapy]]. She dismisses population-segmented fitness marketing (menopause-specific workout or protein rules) as repackaging of unchanged fundamentals, and prefers big-lever prioritization — three meals with protein, movement, outdoor time, human connection, screen rest — over optimization minutiae, with her practical anti-perfectionism example being declining her dentist's thrice-daily timed-brushing request as a risk she is willing to tolerate. Her formative anecdote runs the other direction of the usual physician story: as a medical student with unexplained illness and normal exhaustive testing, the answer was severe anxiety and undereating found through therapy she sought herself — the origin of her claim that patients hold more data on themselves than any workup captures. ## Practical implications

- Use her material as practice-grounded clinical judgment and communication frameworks rather than outcome evidence; her strongest factual claims (HRT as default standard of care; 100 million Americans without primary-care access) run ahead of guidance and need verification.
- Promotional context: appearances coincide with her book launch; positions favoring her care model (long-visit primary care) align with her practice's business model.

## Gaps & open questions

- Do question-first consultation frameworks measurably change outcomes, adherence, or low-value testing compared with standard visits?
- What is the verified figure for Americans lacking primary-care access, and how does it trend?
