# Federica Amati

Federica Amati is ZOE's head nutritionist, a scientist at Imperial College London, a clinical nutritionist by background, and author of *Every Body Should Know This* and *The Appetite Reset*, a book on eating before, during, and after GLP-1 therapy. She works alongside prescribers rather than prescribing herself. Her distinctive contribution to the corpus is a structured nutritional framework around GLP-1 medication: a preparatory fiber-focused phase before starting, lowest-effective-dose titration, diet-first management of gastrointestinal side effects, protein and nutrient-density priorities during treatment, and a deliberate tapering-plus-high-volume-food strategy for coming off. (@joinzoe (ZOE) — "The Nutrition Doctor: “You’re at risk of becoming malnourished!” How to avoid Ozempic's hidden risks", 2026-06-18, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GwZhDggykMQ))

## Evidence stance and distinctive positions

She frames GLP-1 drugs as brain and fat-loss drugs rather than cosmetic weight-loss drugs, and treats food noise — persistent, pervasive, sometimes ruminative food preoccupation — as one of their most transformative targets, while insisting the drugs reduce appetite but cannot repair a poor diet. Recurring positions: undernourishment (micronutrient deficiency, dehydration, lean-mass loss) is the underappreciated risk when intake drops 30–40%; roughly nine in ten users access the drugs without dietary, exercise, or psychological support, which she treats as a system failure and a regulation gap (including compounded and online-pharmacy access); protein targets rise on treatment but are, in her telling, pointless without resistance training; and appetite physiology is best taught as a four-stage cascade (cephalic, gastric stretch, small-intestinal nutrient sensing, colonic fermentation) that whole foods engage and ultra-processed foods bypass. She distinguishes patients with genuine metabolic indications from vanity microdosing, and allows that some patients — particularly with multi-generational obesity — may need the drugs long term, while reporting patients of her own who maintained fat loss for two years after structured tapering. ## Practical implications

- Treat her GLP-1 framework as expert clinical-nutrition practice grounded in trial and real-world side-effect data but not itself validated as a protocol in randomized comparisons; specific numbers (protein g/kg, phase durations) are recorded with those limits at [[glp-1-receptor-agonists]].
- Weigh commercial context: she is a ZOE employee and book author, and her appearances promote ZOE products (Daily30, the Gut Health Bar) alongside the science.

## Gaps & open questions

- Does her pre-drug dietary-preparation phase measurably reduce GI side effects, discontinuation, or dose requirements in a controlled design?
- Do her tapering-plus-high-volume-diet maintenance cases generalize beyond selected, supported patients?
