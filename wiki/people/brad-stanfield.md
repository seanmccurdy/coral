# Brad Stanfield

Brad Stanfield is a family medicine doctor who communicates the science of preventive care, with a consistent method across the corpus: lead with the randomized-trial numbers including absolute risks, name the enrolled population before extrapolating, put diet, exercise, and sleep ahead of drugs, and translate trial protocols into clinic-usable routines (home blood-pressure protocols, frailty screens, protein targets). He routinely distinguishes relative from absolute risk and flags when a headline effect (SPRINT's 25% relative reduction) is about half a percentage point per year in absolute terms. (@DrBradStanfield (Dr Brad Stanfield) — "The 120/80 Blood Pressure Target Is Wrong", 2026-07-20, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4vytb0MOE6M))

Two positions are distinctively his rather than consensus. First, a strong patient-autonomy stance on screening: doctors should present the real numbers and then support the patient's choice — he opposes recommending unproven screening such as whole-body MRI (and condemns referral-kickback arrangements with MRI clinics as unacceptable conflicts of interest), yet also argues doctors should not act as barriers to a fully informed patient, and he underwent a whole-body MRI himself against the American College of Radiology's recommendation. Second, a time-series hypothesis for screening's future: as scans become cheap and fast, replace one-shot detection with repeated imaging and deliberate wait-and-watch, breaking the reflexive biopsy-and-surgery cascade — explicitly acknowledged as untested. [[cancer-screening-and-overdiagnosis]] (@DrBradStanfield (Dr Brad Stanfield) — "'Lie to Your Doctor'", 2026-07-23, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZSLxwF1h9ik))

His clinical framing recurs across pages: blood-pressure targets set by frailty rather than age ([[blood-pressure-targets-and-frailty]]); satiety-first fat loss working with appetite biology rather than willpower, with epicardial fat as the depot of interest and SGLT2 inhibitors as the pharmacologic development he is most enthusiastic about within indications ([[visceral-and-ectopic-fat]], [[sglt2-inhibitors]]); and medications framed as adjuncts that are never a replacement for diet, exercise, and sleep. (@DrBradStanfield (Dr Brad Stanfield) — "$7 Pill Shrinks the Fat Around Your Heart", 2026-07-15, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jn-HKoOUd5o))

His supplement analysis centers on indication, population, dose, formulation, and clinical endpoint rather than nutrient reputation. That method leads him to reject high-dose antioxidant intuition after harmful vitamin C, beta-carotene, and vitamin E trials, while preserving the disease-specific AREDS2 formulation as a counterexample. He now treats the small randomized COSMOS cognitive signal as enough to reject the universal expensive-urine dismissal, but not as proof of cancer, cardiovascular, dementia, or survival prevention; his criticism that the 2026 WHO dementia guidance did not reassess supplementation is a distinct evidence-currency position, complicated by his disclosed commercial interest in supplement formulation. It also makes him more cautious after a positive K2 coronary-calcium trial because calcification is an ambiguous surrogate and no cardiovascular-event benefit has been shown. A more contrarian clinical position appears in gout care: he proposes that weight loss on GLP-1 therapy can make allopurinol unnecessary for some patients and should trigger dose reduction when urate becomes very low. Medication review during major weight change is sound, but GLP-1 therapy has not superseded guideline-preferred allopurinol, and no universal lower urate stopping threshold is established. [[gout-and-urate-lowering]] (@DrBradStanfield (Dr Brad Stanfield) — "I’m Tired of Gout Not Being Treated Properly", 2026-07-13, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hz7EwSQuObw))

His GLP-1 risk-communication position separates signal detection from causal public warning. He rejects David Sinclair's claim that the drugs have doubled sudden blindness in the general population because the initiating NAION report came from a selected specialist clinic, contained few exposed cases, was vulnerable to shared metabolic risk and diagnostic miscoding, and could not establish causality. His practical stance is vigilance for sudden vision loss without reflexive discontinuation in asymptomatic patients; he disclosed personal low-dose tirzepatide use and no GLP-1 financial interest. [[glp-1-receptor-agonists]] (@DrBradStanfield (Dr Brad Stanfield) — "David Sinclair Says Popular Peptide Causes Blindness", 2026-08-20, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C-Twr2WlZCA))

Two later integrations extend the method to population nutrition and oncology. On iodine, he argues the US is quietly reversing history's most cost-effective cognitive intervention — salt iodization — as specialty salts, non-iodized processed-food salt, and the loss of accidental dairy and bread iodine erode intake; his fix is characteristically dose-bounded (RDA-level supplementation, third-party-tested products, guideline-aligned 150 µg in pregnancy) and explicitly refuses the obvious vehicle, because salt reduction has stronger outcome evidence than iodine repletion. That last move — recommending less of the fortified carrier while replacing its nutrient — is a clean example of his substitution-not-addition reasoning. [[iodine-and-cognitive-development]] (@DrBradStanfield (Dr Brad Stanfield) — "Our IQ Will Start Dropping Soon (new evidence)", 2026-08-28, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CfrE9yZhYFs)) On the neoantigen melanoma vaccine, he separates a mechanism he finds convincing (central-tolerance escape, which he expects to generalize across tumor types) from evidence he grades cautiously — flagging that the phase 3 win is a press-release endpoint without published numbers and reading the 177% single-day stock move as priced-in hope of generalization rather than demonstrated benefit. ## Practical implications

- Treat his material as evidence-explained clinical communication with a general-practice lens; his videos consistently carry the disclaimer that he is not the viewer's physician, and his protocol suggestions (home BP measurement, frailty screening, protein targets) should be checked against local guidance. (@DrBradStanfield (Dr Brad Stanfield) — "The 120/80 Blood Pressure Target Is Wrong", 2026-07-20, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4vytb0MOE6M))
- His screening-autonomy position is an attributed stance in live professional dispute — the informed-consent statement he reads against whole-body MRI screening coexists with his personal decision to be scanned; the wiki records both sides rather than resolving them. (@DrBradStanfield (Dr Brad Stanfield) — "'Lie to Your Doctor'", 2026-07-23, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZSLxwF1h9ik))

## Gaps & open questions

- He sells personalized health plans through his site; the corpus has not examined whether that commercial interest shapes which interventions his videos emphasize.
- How representative are his clinic-derived heuristics (four-question frailty screen, first-day-discard home BP averaging) of validated instruments?
- How often do his proposed conservative supplement doses or deprescribing thresholds come from outcome evidence rather than risk-minimizing clinical judgment?
