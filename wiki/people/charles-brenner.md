# Charles Brenner

Charles Brenner is a biochemist (professor and chair, Department of Diabetes and Cancer Metabolism, City of Hope) who discovered the nicotinamide riboside (NR) kinase pathway — the route by which NR enters cells and is phosphorylated toward NAD — and whose technology seeded the commercial NAD-boosting industry. The discovery came from enzymology rather than aging research: while working on glutamine-dependent NAD synthetase (explaining a glutamine requirement first purified by Preiss and Handler's circle in 1958), he noticed the canonical NAD-synthesis diagrams predicted his yeast knockout should be unable to make NAD, hypothesized an undescribed nucleoside route, and set up the experiment that found the NR pathway. (FoundMyFitness — "How To Boost NAD Levels To Fight Inflammation, Improve Recovery, and Slow Aging", 2026-02-10, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELcVYRJJdK4))

**Conflicts of interest, disclosed:** chief scientific adviser of Niagen (the dominant commercial NR) and of the NAD-testing company NADMed, and co-author on several of the NR clinical trials he cites. Every pro-NR claim he makes should be read against these interests; this wiki treats his trial summaries as expert interpretation with commercial interest pending independent verification. (FoundMyFitness — "How To Boost NAD Levels To Fight Inflammation, Improve Recovery, and Slow Aging", 2026-02-10, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELcVYRJJdK4))

## Evidence stance and distinctive positions

His scientific persona is deliberately anti-hype, and several of his sharpest positions cut against his own commercial incentives, which raises their credibility: he denies that human blood NAD shows a general age-related decline (locating disturbance instead in specific tissues under disease states — infection, heart failure, alcoholic liver, over-nutrition, neurodegeneration, circadian disruption); he sees no consumer use case for NAD blood testing despite advising a testing company, comparing it to mass-speccing yourself to confirm you swallowed an aspirin; and he criticizes trial designs he was part of, calling the grip-strength and 13-week-monotherapy-weight-loss endpoints unreasonable, and explaining the multiplicity problem with secondary-endpoint findings. (FoundMyFitness — "How To Boost NAD Levels To Fight Inflammation, Improve Recovery, and Slow Aging", 2026-02-10, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELcVYRJJdK4))

Attributed contrarian positions in the current corpus: the resveratrol/sirtuin longevity story is unsupported — he holds there is no evidence basis for calling SIRT1 a longevity gene, that resveratrol and pterostilbene do not actually activate it, and that resveratrol antagonized NR's benefit in the peripheral-artery-disease randomized trial; NAD IV drips lack a delivery rationale because phosphorylated compounds cannot enter cells, making the multi-hour, innate-immune-provoking infusion at best a costly precursor-delivery route; oral NAD and NMN reduce to NR delivery after extracellular breakdown, deflating NMN's "one step closer" marketing; and he resists one-size-fits-all podcast prescriptions such as universal fasting or time-restricted eating, preferring condition-matched interventions (GLP-1 medication plus resistance training for people with obesity). Where he is bullish — NR as proven anti-inflammatory in humans, a plausible first indication in peripheral artery disease, and use cases in recovery and infection resilience — the claims and their conflicts are weighed at [[nad-supplementation]] and [[nad-precursors-and-healthy-aging]]. (FoundMyFitness — "How To Boost NAD Levels To Fight Inflammation, Improve Recovery, and Slow Aging", 2026-02-10, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELcVYRJJdK4))

## Practical implications

- Use his material as the field's best mechanistic teaching on NAD biology ([[nad-metabolism]]) and as an insider's map of the NR trial base, while discounting benefit claims for the manufacturer he advises; his against-interest statements (no blood-NAD decline, no consumer testing, anti-IV, anti-resveratrol) are more independently credible than his pro-NR use-case claims.

## Gaps & open questions

- Which of the NR trials he summarizes hold up on independent reading of populations, endpoints, and effect sizes, without co-author involvement?
